Most group trips to New Orleans don't fail on the ground. They fail in the group chat, months earlier, because everyone is arguing about restaurants before anyone has picked a weekend.

The fix is boring: order of operations. There is a correct sequence to planning a big-group trip here, and every step gets easier if the one before it is actually locked. This page is that sequence. Each step links to a deeper guide when you're ready to go down the rabbit hole — but if you only read one page on this site, read this one, do the steps in order, and you'll be ahead of 90% of groups.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Decide New Orleans is the destination (or settle the debate — see step 0)
- [ ] Pick dates against the festival calendar, not around them by accident
- [ ] Lock the headcount with money, not emojis
- [ ] Book where you're sleeping — this is the scarce resource, not flights
- [ ] Have the budget conversation once, early, in writing
- [ ] Make 2–3 big-table dinner reservations
- [ ] Only then build the itinerary
- [ ] Week of: sort ground transport and send one info dump

## Step 0: Settle the destination argument

If the group is still torn between New Orleans and somewhere else, don't litigate it in the chat — the comparisons are already written. We've put New Orleans head-to-head with [Nashville](/guides/nola-group-new-orleans-vs-nashville-guide/), [Austin](/guides/nola-group-new-orleans-vs-austin-guide/), [Charleston](/guides/nola-group-new-orleans-vs-charleston-guide/), [Savannah](/guides/nola-group-new-orleans-vs-savannah-guide/), and [Las Vegas](/guides/nola-group-new-orleans-vs-las-vegas-guide/), among others. Send the relevant one to the group and let it do the arguing.

The short version of every comparison: New Orleans wins on culture-per-dollar and walkability, loses on rooftop-pool weather in July, and is the only city on the list where the entertainment comes to you — sometimes literally, in the form of a brass band rolling past your dinner.

## Step 1: Pick the dates — first, and against the calendar

Dates come first because everything else — price, availability, what the trip even feels like — hangs off them. New Orleans runs on an [inverted calendar](/guides/nola-group-best-time-to-visit-guide/): October is peak, spring is glorious and busy, and summer is the sweaty bargain. A weekend that looks random on your calendar might be [Mardi Gras, Jazz Fest, or Essence](/events/) here, which can double lodging prices — or be exactly the point of the trip.

So before anyone votes on dates, check the [festival and events calendar](/events/). Landing on a festival weekend by accident is the single most expensive planning mistake a group makes. Landing on one on purpose can be the whole reason to come.

If you're eyeing August or September, read the [hurricane season guide](/guides/nola-group-hurricane-season-guide/) first — not to scare you off, but so you book refundable where it counts.

## Step 2: Lock the headcount with money

"Who's in?" produces enthusiasm. Deposits produce a headcount. The gap between the two is where group trips go to die.

The number matters more here than in most cities, because [10, 15, 20, and 30 people are genuinely different trips](/guides/group-size-guide/) in New Orleans — different lodging categories, different restaurant strategies, different transport math. You need a real number before step 3, and the only way to get one is to collect money early. The [first-time organizer playbook](/guides/nola-group-first-time-organizer-guide/) covers how to run that conversation without losing friends, including what to do about the person who drops out three weeks before the trip (someone will).

## Step 3: Book the beds — this is the scarce resource

Flights to New Orleans are a commodity. Places that sleep eighteen people under one roof are not. Large-format lodging is the genuine bottleneck of a big-group trip here, and the good options book out months ahead for prime weekends.

Start with the [where-to-stay overview](/where-to-stay/), then work the two real decisions: [hotel versus big house](/guides/hotel-vs-villa-guide/) — separate bills and daily housekeeping versus one roof and a shared kitchen — and [which neighborhood](/guides/nola-group-neighborhood-comparison-guide/), because the French Quarter is where you'll visit, not necessarily where you should sleep. Groups that book beds before dinner reservations think they planned early. Groups that book beds before *dates are even final* have it backwards — see step 1.

## Step 4: Have the money conversation once, in writing

One conversation, early, covering the three numbers everyone silently disagrees about: lodging per person, the food-and-drink expectation, and what's covered by the group pot versus everyone for themselves. The [budget guide](/guides/budget-guide/) has line-by-line math at three spending levels — send it before the conversation so the argument is about numbers, not vibes.

Then set up one shared payment thread and [track it as you go](/guides/nola-group-budget-tracking-mid-trip-guide/). The trip where "we'll settle up after" works is a trip for four people, not fourteen.

## Step 5: Book 2–3 dinners before you build anything else

A table for eighteen on a Saturday night in New Orleans is a reservation made weeks out, not a walk-in. It's also the skeleton of your trip: once two or three big dinners are on the books, the days plan themselves around them.

The [restaurant guide](/guides/restaurant-guide/) covers which places genuinely handle large parties — including private dining rooms — and which beloved spots will never seat your crew no matter how nicely you ask. Book the dinners, then stop. You don't need every meal planned; you need the anchor meals locked.

## Step 6: Now — and only now — the itinerary

The itinerary comes last because it's the most flexible piece and the one people most enjoy over-planning first. With dates, beds, budget, and dinners locked, use the [itinerary builder framework](/guides/group-itinerary-builder-guide/) to structure the days — or start from the [3-day itinerary](/guides/itinerary-3-day/) and adapt.

The one structural rule for groups: plan mornings loosely and evenings firmly, and never schedule the whole group for the whole day. Splitting up for an afternoon isn't a planning failure — it's the thing that keeps twenty people liking each other by day three.

## The week before: logistics, then one info dump

Ground transport is the last real decision — how eighteen people move between a house, a restaurant, and a second line without a 45-minute rideshare shuffle. The [transportation guide](/guides/transportation-guide/) does the van-versus-rideshare math.

Then send one message with everything in it — address, door codes, dinner times, weather, packing notes. The [welcome packet guide](/guides/group-welcome-packet-guide/) has a template, and the [safety guide](/guides/nola-group-safety-guide/) covers the handful of real rules worth including. One info dump beats forty questions in the chat, and it's the difference between an organizer who enjoys the trip and one who works it.
