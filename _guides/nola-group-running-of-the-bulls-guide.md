New Orleans took Pamplona's most famous tradition and did the only sensible thing with it: replaced the goring livestock with roller-derby skaters swinging plastic bats. San Fermín in Nola — the city's Running of the Bulls — is exactly as ridiculous and exactly as fun as that sounds, and it happens to be one of the better organized-chaos activities you can point a big group at all summer.

Here's the honest framing. This is not a spectator festival you watch from the sidelines. It's a participatory street event: hundreds of people in white with red sashes run a route through the CBD and Warehouse District while "bulls" — costumed skaters — chase them and swat at them. It's a costume party, a fun run, and a block party stacked on top of each other, and it lands in mid-July, right in the same soft-season demand pocket as [Tales of the Cocktail](/guides/nola-group-tales-of-the-cocktail-guide/).

For a crew of ten to thirty, that's close to ideal. Everyone has a role, the buy-in is low, and the whole thing is built around drinking and moving together. The catch is the same catch as every July event here: the heat is real, the crowd is thick, and a big group that doesn't set a meetup plan will scatter inside of ten minutes.

- [ ] Reserve any group meal for after the run, not during — nobody eats mid-chaos

## What San Fermín in Nola Actually Is

Strip the costume off it and there are three parts to the day, usually running morning into afternoon:

**The opening.** Modeled on Pamplona's kickoff, there's a ceremonial start — speeches, a countdown, a whole lot of people in white packed together and getting louder. This is the pre-game, and it's where your group wants to already be together, because once the run starts, together is over.

**The run itself.** The "bulls" — roller-derby skaters in horned helmets swinging wiffle-style bats — chase runners along a set route through the CBD and Warehouse District. It is not a race and there's no finish-line glory. The point is to get gently swatted, laugh about it, and keep moving. Contact is part of the deal; if someone in your group doesn't want to get whacked with a plastic bat, they should spectate, not run.

**The after-party.** The run empties out into a block party with music, drinks, and a costume-heavy crowd cooling off. This is where a scattered group reconvenes, and it's the reason the meetup plan matters more than anything else you'll decide.

> Don't trust any date, route, or start-time detail from memory — this event's specifics move year to year. Confirm everything on the official San Fermín in Nola site before you book flights or lock a plan. This guide is about how to run a group through the day, not what this year's route is.

## Runners vs. Spectators: Split Your Group

The single best planning move is to accept that not everyone wants to get chased through the CBD in July, and that's fine. A big group naturally splits into two camps, and both have a good day.

| Group member | Runner or spectator? | Why |
|--------------|----------------------|-----|
| The instigators | Runner | This is the entire reason they came; let them go hard |
| Costume-committed but heat-wary | Spectator | Full white-and-red, but watching from shade with a cold drink |
| The day-drinkers | Spectator | The block-party energy is the real draw for them |
| Anyone with a bad knee or ankle | Spectator | It's a crowded run on city streets; skip the sprained-ankle risk |
| The photographers | Spectator | Best shots come from the sidelines, not the scrum |

Send the runners off, station the spectators along the route or at the after-party, and give everyone the same reconvene point. Nobody feels dragged into something they didn't want, and the group still shares the day.

## The Dress Code Is Non-Negotiable (and Practical)

White clothes, red sash at the waist, red bandana at the neck. It's borrowed straight from Pamplona, and in New Orleans in July it happens to be the smartest thing you could wear anyway — white reflects the sun, loose cotton breathes, and the red accents are cheap and easy to source in bulk for a group.

A few group-logistics notes:

- **Buy the sashes and bandanas as a batch.** One person orders twenty of each online ahead of the trip and it's done. Scrambling day-of is a bad use of a July morning.
- **White you don't love.** It's going to get sweat-through, drink-splashed, and bat-smudged. Nobody should wear anything they're precious about.
- **Comfortable closed-toe shoes for runners.** This is a crowded run on real pavement. Sandals are how ankles get rolled.

## The July Reality: Heat and Timing

This is the part out-of-town groups underestimate. Mid-July in New Orleans is genuinely punishing — high heat, high humidity, and a near-daily chance of an afternoon thunderstorm. The run is a physical, crowded, sun-exposed activity in the worst-weather month of the year.

That's not a reason to skip it. It's a reason to plan the heat like it's the main event, because for your group's stamina, it is.

- **Hydrate before, during, and after.** Water between every drink. This is the difference between a group that makes it to the after-party and one that's tapped out by noon.
- **Sunscreen the whole crew before you leave the house.** There's no shade on a run route.
- **Have an AC recovery plan.** Where does the group go to cool down after — a bar, a restaurant, back to the base? Decide before, not during.
- **Watch the sky.** An afternoon storm can roll through fast. It usually passes, but a big group needs a "where do we duck in" instinct.

Worth remembering: July is otherwise New Orleans' soft season — thinner crowds and lower rates than the October and spring peaks — but event weekends like this one and Tales of the Cocktail spike locally inside that soft month. ## Getting the Group There and Back

The route closes streets in the CBD and Warehouse District, which scrambles the normal transportation math. Two things to plan around:

- **Rideshare surges and gets slow near the route.** Dropping twenty people at the start line by app is a bad plan. Aim for a nearby edge and walk the last few blocks together, or stay somewhere close enough to walk the whole way.
- **The group will not leave as one unit.** Runners, spectators, and diehards finish at wildly different times. Set the reconvene point, then let people find their own way back from there.

## A Loose Shape for the Day

Not a rigid schedule — a rhythm that survives a hot, chaotic, participatory event.

| Time | Move |
|------|------|
| Morning | Group breakfast at the base, sunscreen, everyone in white-and-red |
| Late morning | Head to the start area together, well before the countdown |
| Midday | The run — runners go, spectators post up along the route or at the finish |
| Early afternoon | Reconvene at the fixed meetup point; hydrate hard |
| Afternoon | After-party — music, drinks, cooling off, comparing bat welts |
| Evening | AC recovery, a real group meal, then optional nightlife for whoever's left |

The point is one anchored meetup and a lot of opt-in around it. A big group can't run this day on a single rigid plan, and it doesn't need to.

If your group has done Jazz Fest or French Quarter Fest, reset your expectations. Those are music festivals with fixed grounds and a clear center of gravity. San Fermín in Nola is a moving street event — a costume, a route, and a party, not a field with stages. It's closer in spirit to a themed second line than to a ticketed festival.

It also sits in an unusual calendar slot: a marquee summer event in a month most people write off. ## Pro Tips

1. **Assign one meetup captain.** One person owns the reconvene point and time and repeats it until everyone's sick of hearing it. On a day this scattered, that person saves the group.

2. **Batch-order the sashes and bandanas.** Twenty of each, one order, done weeks ahead. Do not make this a day-of scramble.

3. **Runners run, everyone else drinks in the shade.** Forcing the whole crew to run is how you end up with a heatstroke case and a sulking spectator. Let the split happen naturally.

4. **Hydrate like it's the job.** Water between every drink, no exceptions. July heat plus a run plus a block party is a dehydration machine.

5. **Don't schedule anything tight for right after.** The group will trickle out of the after-party over hours. Leave the afternoon loose and set dinner for the evening.

6. **Book any group dinner well ahead.** It's a festival weekend in a popular month; walk-in for twenty won't happen. Reserve the moment you have dates.

7. **Keep the precious stuff at home.** White you don't love, shoes that can take a beating, nothing you'd miss if it got splashed, smudged, or lost in the crowd.
