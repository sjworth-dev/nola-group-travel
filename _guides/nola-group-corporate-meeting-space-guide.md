A party venue and a working meeting venue are not the same booking. A courtyard buyout gets your group drinks and a DJ. It does not get you a screen that works, a room that stays quiet for three hours, or chairs anyone can sit in past 11 AM.

If your trip is actually a working meeting — a sales kickoff, a leadership offsite with a formal agenda, a board session — you need a venue built for that job. New Orleans has plenty of it. Most of it lives inside hotels, not inside the courtyard bars and private dining rooms that dominate the rest of this site's event coverage.

This guide is about that specific booking: hotel meeting and conference space for a large working group, and the questions that keep it from going wrong.

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Confirm this is a meeting booking, not a social event booking — the two are sold by different departments at most hotels
- [ ] Get total square footage and a room diagram, not just a stated capacity
- [ ] Ask whether the quote is day-rate, F&B-minimum, or both
- [ ] Confirm AV is included or itemized separately — screens, mics, and a house tech are not always bundled
- [ ] Ask about breakout room availability if your agenda splits into smaller groups
- [ ] Get WiFi specs in writing: bandwidth, whether it's shared with other hotel events that day
- [ ] Confirm the room hold — some venues release your block if the F&B minimum isn't signed by a deadline
- [ ] Book 6+ months out for the largest selection of dates and room configurations

## Meeting Space vs. Party Venue vs. Room Block

These three get confused constantly, and each is sold differently.

**Meeting/conference space** is a room rented for a working agenda — presentations, breakout sessions, structured time. Priced by the day or half-day, sometimes bundled with catering, sometimes not.

**Party/event space** — buyout bars, private dining rooms, courtyards — is built for a social event, not a workday. The [private event spaces guide](/guides/private-event-spaces-guide/) covers that booking in full: minimums, capacity, neighborhood options.

**Room block** is just sleeping rooms held at a group rate. It says nothing about where your group will actually meet during the day. Plenty of teams book a solid room block and then discover the hotel's only meeting room is a converted lobby nook.

Confirm which one you're booking before you get a quote. Sales teams at large hotels often have separate contacts for each.

## Where Meeting Space Actually Lives

New Orleans' formal meeting inventory concentrates in a few places:

**Full-service hotels with dedicated meeting floors.** Properties like The Roosevelt run actual conference-and-banquet operations — multiple room sizes, in-house AV, catering staff who do this daily. This is the deepest, most reliable inventory for a group that needs guaranteed square footage and a professional operation behind it.

**Boutique and mid-size hotels with one or two flexible rooms.** Smaller full-service properties often have a single meeting room or a private dining room that converts for daytime use. Fine for a team of 20-40 with a simple agenda; tight if you need simultaneous breakouts.

**The Ernest N. Morial Convention Center**, if your event is genuinely convention-scale. That's a different booking process entirely and outside the scope of most trips this size — see the [convention group guide](/guides/convention-group-guide/) if you're attending, rather than hosting, a large-scale event there.

**What doesn't work as a substitute:** a private villa's living room, no matter how large. Villas are excellent for informal work sessions with a small team — the [corporate offsite productivity guide](/guides/corporate-offsite-productivity-guide/) covers exactly that format — but a villa is not a meeting-room booking. There's no house AV, no dedicated tech support, and no fire-code capacity rating. For a session under 20 people with an informal agenda, that's a feature. For a formal presentation to 60 people, it's a problem.

## Room Configurations and What They're For

| Setup | Best for | Rough capacity feel |
|-------|----------|---------------------|
| Theater (rows facing front) | Presentations, keynotes, all-hands | Highest headcount per square foot |
| Classroom (tables + chairs facing front) | Training, note-taking sessions | Fewer people, more comfort |
| Boardroom (single large table) | Leadership meetings, small working groups | Lowest headcount, most collaborative |
| U-shape | Discussion-heavy sessions, workshops | Moderate headcount, high engagement |
| Breakout rooms (multiple smaller rooms) | Splitting into working groups | Depends on room count |

Ask the venue which configurations their room actually supports — not every room can be flipped between setups on the same day, and reset time between configurations eats into your agenda if you don't plan for it.

## Pricing: Day-Rate vs. F&B Minimum

Hotels sell meeting space one of three ways, and it's worth knowing which you're being quoted:

**Flat day rate.** You pay a set fee for the room for the day. Catering, AV, and staffing are quoted separately.

**F&B minimum, room included.** The room itself is "free" as long as your group's food and beverage spend hits a set minimum. This is common when a hotel wants to fill the room with a catering booking rather than a bare rental.

**Day rate plus F&B minimum.** Both apply. More common for larger rooms or peak dates.

None of these has a fixed dollar figure worth publishing here — it varies by property, room size, date, and what's bundled. Get an itemized quote and ask directly which model you're being charged under before you compare two venues, because a lower "room fee" from one property can hide a steeper F&B minimum than a competitor's all-in day rate.

## AV and Tech: What to Confirm Before You Sign

This is where meeting bookings go wrong most often. Ask specifically:

1. **Is a screen and projector/display included, or itemized?** Some venues bundle basic AV into the room fee; others charge a separate line for every screen and mic.
2. **Is there a house AV tech on-site during your meeting**, or is a rented system dropped off with no support?
3. **What's the WiFi bandwidth**, and is it dedicated to your room or shared across the whole property's events that day? For a team live-streaming to remote colleagues or running heavy video, shared hotel WiFi during a busy weekend can be a real bottleneck.
4. **Can you bring outside AV vendors**, or is the venue's in-house provider mandatory? Mandatory in-house AV is common and not unreasonable — just price it before you assume you'll bring your own gear.
5. **Is there a backup mic and a backup laptop connection?** Ask what happens if the primary system fails mid-presentation.

## Catering for an All-Day Working Session

Working-meeting catering has different needs than a dinner event: it has to keep people focused through a full agenda, not slow them down.

**Morning:** Coffee service that's actually adequate for the headcount — understocked coffee for a room of 30 is a recurring complaint. Light breakfast (pastries, fruit) that doesn't require utensils or a sit-down break.

**Midday:** A working lunch that can be eaten at the table if the agenda is tight, or a short break if it isn't. Buffet service is faster to move through than plated for a large group with a schedule to hold.

**Afternoon:** A snack/coffee refresh around the 2-3 PM energy dip. This is the block most agendas forget and most attendees remember.

**Ask the venue directly** whether catering is in-house-only or whether outside catering is permitted — this varies by property and affects both cost and menu flexibility.

## For Large Groups

The venue booking is only half the trip. Once the meeting space is locked in, you still need to house the team — and for a group in the double digits, that's its own decision separate from where you meet during the day.

Some teams book a room block at the same hotel hosting the meeting — properties like The Roosevelt or Hotel Peter & Paul run both meeting space and group room blocks, which is the convenience play if walking downstairs matters more than anything else. Others split the day: formal meeting space at a hotel, then a private villa as the group's actual base for meals and evenings. Villas in the Bywater and Lower Garden District — Castleday Retreats and The Syd among them — offer more room to spread out and no F&B minimums for anything outside the working sessions. The [corporate retreat guide](/guides/corporate-retreat/) walks through that villa-vs-hotel calculation in full, including where private villas outperform hotel rooms for group cohesion once the workday ends.

Either way, don't assume the hotel with the best meeting rooms also has the best group lodging value — price the two separately.

## Pro Tips

1. **Site-visit the actual room, not a similar one.** Hotel sales decks show their best room. Ask specifically which room your date and headcount will get, and see it before signing.

2. **Confirm the room hold deadline.** Meeting space often gets released back to general sales if a signed contract and deposit aren't in by a stated date — know that date before you assume the room is yours.

3. **Ask what's happening in the hotel that same day.** A wedding block or another conference sharing the property can affect elevator traffic, WiFi load, and parking. Not disqualifying, just worth knowing.

4. **Separate the meeting quote from the room-block quote**, even if you're booking both at the same property. Bundling them can obscure which line item is actually driving the total cost.

5. **Build break time generously.** A packed agenda that ignores transition time between sessions, meals, and bathroom breaks will run late by 2 PM.

6. **Get the cancellation policy for the meeting space specifically** — it's frequently different from the sleeping-room cancellation policy at the same property.

7. **If your agenda has any evening social component, book it as a separate line.** Don't assume the meeting-space quote includes an evening reception; that's usually priced and contracted separately, closer to the [private event spaces guide](/guides/private-event-spaces-guide/) territory.
