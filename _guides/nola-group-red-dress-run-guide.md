The name undersells it. The Red Dress Run is billed as a run. It is, generously, a walk. What it actually is: thousands of people, every gender, in a red dress, moving slowly through the French Quarter and Marigny between bars, raising money for local charities along the way. Nobody's timing a mile split.

It's organized by the local Hash House Harriers chapter — a running-club-adjacent drinking club whose motto is famously "a drinking club with a running problem" — and it lands every August, squarely in New Orleans' soft season. That timing is not an accident for a group planning around it: rates are down, the city is thinner on tourists, and this is one of the few genuinely large-scale group events happening in that window.

For a big group, it's close to a perfect anchor: a costume everyone can pull off cheaply, a route that's really a rolling bar crawl, a charity angle that gives the day some weight, and a season where lodging isn't fighting you. Here's how to run it.

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Confirm the exact date, start time, and staging point on the official Hash House Harriers New Orleans site — it moves within August year to year
- [ ] Get everyone a red dress before the trip — thrift stores, costume shops, or ordered online; sizing and availability get tighter closer to the date
- [ ] Register in advance if the event requires it; registration often bundles in a charity donation
- [ ] Decide as a group how much of the route you're actually doing — the "run" is really a bar-to-bar crawl, and nobody has to do the whole thing
- [ ] Hydrate deliberately — this is a daytime August event, and a day of drinking in the heat is a different animal than a night out
- [ ] Set a rally point and a check-in time before the group scatters between bars
- [ ] Bring cash for the bars along the route and for any on-site charity donations

## What the Red Dress Run Actually Is

Strip away the dresses and it's a charity pub crawl with a costume requirement and a nominal "run" framing borrowed from the Hash House Harriers' running-club roots.

**The organizer.** The Hash House Harriers are an international running-club tradition built around the joke that the running is really an excuse to drink together afterward. The New Orleans chapter runs this event annually, and the proceeds go to local charities — a genuine philanthropic backbone under the costume party.

**The dress code.** Everyone — every gender — wears a red dress. That's the whole rule. Some people go for full costume commitment, wigs and all; others grab the cheapest red dress they can find and call it done. Both are correct. The point is a sea of red moving through the [French Quarter](/neighborhoods/french-quarter/) and [Marigny](/neighborhoods/marigny/), not a judged costume contest.

**The "run."** A route winds through the Quarter and Marigny, hitting bars along the way. Some people jog stretches of it; most people walk it at a pace that accommodates a drink in hand. It's a moving street party more than an athletic event.

**The charity angle.** Registration and on-site donations support local causes — check the current year's beneficiaries on the official site. It's a real fundraiser wrapped in an absurd costume, which is a very New Orleans combination.

> Don't lock in a specific date, start time, or route from memory — confirm all of it on the official Hash House Harriers New Orleans site before you build a trip around it.

## The August Timing Is the Whole Case

New Orleans' summer — June through August — is soft season: heat and humidity push rates down and thin out the crowds, with a couple of sharp exceptions carved out by specific events. The Red Dress Run is one of those exceptions, but it's a demand blip inside an otherwise value month, not a citywide sellout the way Mardi Gras or Jazz Fest are.

That combination is useful for a group: a genuinely large, well-attended event, layered on top of a season where hotel and villa rates haven't spiked the way they do in spring. A group that wants a big-energy weekend without spring pricing has a real case for building around this one.

| Factor | Red Dress Run weekend | Spring festival weekend |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Season | August soft season | February–May peak season |
| Baseline rates | Lower | Higher |
| Event scale | Large, single-day | Large, multi-day |
| Crowd type | Local + regional, costume-driven | Heavy visitor influx |
| Booking pressure | Moderate | High |

That said, "soft season" is a citywide baseline, not a guarantee for this specific weekend — properties that can house a big group are limited enough, year-round, that the standard rule still applies: for the largest selection, book 6+ months out.

## Running It With a Group

### Before: Get the Dresses Sorted Early

This is the one piece of prep that actually needs a lead time. A group of any size scrambling for red dresses the week of the trip is a group paying costume-shop markup for whatever's left. Order or thrift them early, and decide as a group whether you're going full costume or just "technically compliant" — mixed commitment levels are fine, but knowing in advance avoids a last-minute scramble.

### The Route: Decide How Much You're Actually Doing

Nobody has to do the whole route at a run. A big group does better splitting the day honestly:

| Group member | Approach |
|---------------|----------|
| The committed | Do the full route, bar to bar, costume maxed out |
| The social drinkers | Hit a few key stops, skip the rest, meet back up |
| The heat-averse | Watch from a shaded bar patio, join the group between legs |
| Anyone driving later or sitting the day out | Spectate, take photos, meet the group for dinner |

The route runs through dense, walkable [French Quarter](/guides/nola-bourbon-street-guide/) and Marigny streets, so peeling off and rejoining is easy — nobody's stranded the way they might be at a spread-out fairgrounds event.

### Pace the Drinking for an August Day

This is a daytime event in August heat, which changes the math on a day of bar-hopping. Water between stops, real food at some point in the afternoon, and shade breaks aren't optional extras — they're what keeps the group upright for the whole route. Treat it with the same heat discipline as any other outdoor August event.

### After: The Day Doesn't Have to End at the Last Bar

The route winds down into the evening, and a group that's paced itself well still has a full night ahead. [Frenchmen Street](/guides/nola-group-french-quarter-at-night-guide/) is a short walk from the Marigny end of the route for groups who want to keep the night going with live music instead of another bar crawl leg.

## Pro Tips

1. **Order the dresses weeks ahead, not days.** Thrift-store luck runs out fast once the date gets close, and a costume shop scramble the day before is avoidable.

2. **Everyone wears the dress — that's the whole bit.** This isn't an event where half the group opts out of the costume and half goes all in; the sea-of-red visual is what makes it work, and a group that commits together gets better photos and more fun out of it.

3. **Treat it as a bar crawl with a costume, not a race.** Nobody's checking a finish time. Pace the group like you would any long day of drinking, not like an athletic event.

4. **Hydrate like it's a festival day, because it is.** August heat plus a day of drinking is a real combination. Water between every couple of stops.

5. **Bring cash for the route.** Bars along the way and on-site charity donations move faster with cash in hand than a group fumbling for cards.

6. **Wear shoes you can actually walk miles in.** The dress is the costume; the shoes are the part people regret. Comfortable footwear beats a matching heel every time.

7. **Set a hard rally point for the group before you start.** A costume-uniform crowd of thousands makes "just look for us" a bad plan — pick a specific bar or corner and a check-in time.

## For a Big Group

The Red Dress Run is an easy event to run with a big group logistically — it's contained to walkable Quarter and Marigny streets, there's no ticketed gate to funnel people through, and the costume makes a crew easy to spot in a crowd even when it splits up. The strain is the same one every August event in this city carries: heat, a full day outdoors, and the need for a real base to recover at between legs of the route.

A large-format property in or near the Quarter or Marigny keeps the walk to the route short on both ends, and a pool matters more here than at almost any other event on the calendar — a group coming off hours of walking and drinking in August heat needs somewhere to actually cool down before the night continues. Spread the search across the field rather than fixating on one name; fit matters more than brand for a group this size.

For the largest selection, book 6+ months out.
