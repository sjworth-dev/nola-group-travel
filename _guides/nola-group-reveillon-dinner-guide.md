Reveillon means "awakening," and the original version was a late-night feast eaten after midnight Mass on Christmas Eve — Creole New Orleans families breaking a day of fasting with a multi-course meal that ran into the early morning. The tradition faded, then got revived by the city's restaurant scene in the 1990s as something more useful for visitors: a month-long run of fixed-course holiday menus, most of December, at restaurants across the city.

That revival is the version your group will actually experience. Reveillon dinners today are prix-fixe, multi-course menus — usually three to five courses, built around the classic Creole-Christmas dishes — offered as a dinner special for weeks rather than one single night. You don't need to time your trip to Christmas Eve to get the tradition; you need to time it to December.

For a group already building a [holiday gathering](/guides/holiday-gathering/) trip, Reveillon is a strong anchor night: it's a built-in special-occasion dinner that requires no menu-planning on your part, it fits the mood of a December trip, and a fixed multi-course format is genuinely easier to manage for a big table than an à la carte menu with twenty separate orders. Here's how to work it into the trip.

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Confirm which restaurants are running Reveillon menus this December, and their exact date ranges, on each restaurant's own site — not every restaurant participates every year, and the run doesn't always cover the entire month
- [ ] Book well ahead — December is a high-demand month for restaurant reservations city-wide, and a Reveillon table for a full group needs more lead time than a normal dinner
- [ ] Ask directly about large-group capacity when you call — Reveillon menus are usually built around individual tables, and not every restaurant can run the fixed menu for a party of your size
- [ ] Confirm pricing per person before booking; multi-course prix-fixe runs higher than an average dinner and the group should know the number going in
- [ ] Ask about dietary accommodations in advance — a fixed multi-course menu is harder to adjust on the fly than an à la carte one
- [ ] Decide if you want the classic old-line experience or a modern restaurant's take — both exist, and they're different nights out

## What a Reveillon Dinner Actually Is

**The format.** A set multi-course menu — appetizer, soup or salad, a main, and dessert at minimum, sometimes more — offered at a fixed price instead of ordering individually off the regular menu. It's built to move a dining room through a proper holiday meal without the pacing chaos of everyone ordering something different.

**The dishes.** Expect Creole-Christmas staples: gumbo or turtle soup to start, a seafood or game course, a rich main, and a traditional dessert like bread pudding or bûche de Noël. Exact menus vary by restaurant and change year to year — this is one to check on the current menu rather than assume.

**The timing.** Most restaurants run their Reveillon menu across several weeks in December, not just Christmas Eve itself. That gives a group real flexibility on which night of a trip to book it, rather than needing to land in town on one specific date.

**The restaurants.** A wide range of the city's classic and modern kitchens participate in a given year — old-line institutions with decades of Reveillon history alongside newer restaurants that have adopted the format. Which specific restaurants are running it changes annually, so check current participants rather than relying on a past year's list.

| Reveillon dinner | A normal December dinner reservation |
|---|---|
| Fixed multi-course menu, set price | À la carte, per-item ordering |
| Built for a full seated meal, paced by the kitchen | Ordered and paced individually |
| Available for a set run of weeks in December | Available anytime the restaurant is open |
| Often needs advance booking for a full group | Easier to book last-minute for smaller parties |

## Booking It for a Big Group

The fixed-menu format is actually an advantage for a big table — the kitchen already knows what's coming out and when, which is the opposite of the usual large-group restaurant problem where twenty different orders slow a dining room down. See the [large group seating guide](/guides/nola-group-large-group-seating-strategy-guide/) for how that dynamic plays out generally; Reveillon sidesteps most of it by design.

The catch is availability. December is already a busy reservation month across the city, and a Reveillon table large enough for your group needs more advance planning than usual. Call the restaurant directly rather than booking online — ask specifically whether they can run the Reveillon menu for a party your size, whether that means one long table or several tables ordering the same menu, and what the deposit or cancellation policy looks like for a group reservation.

If your group can't land one restaurant with room for everyone, splitting into two tables at the same restaurant running the same menu is a reasonable fallback — you still get the shared experience without needing a single table that fits the whole crew.

## Fitting It Into a Holiday Trip

Reveillon works best as the single formal dinner in an otherwise relaxed December trip — the night the group gets dressed up, in contrast to the [private chef](/guides/private-chef-guide/) or house-cooked nights that fill out the rest of a holiday stay. If your trip is stretching into New Year's, see the [New Year's Eve villa guide](/guides/nola-group-new-years-villa-guide/) for how to structure the back half of a longer December stay.

A few ways it slots into the calendar:

- **Early December trip:** book Reveillon early in the run, before the pre-Christmas rush tightens availability further.
- **Christmas week trip:** expect the highest demand and the tightest tables — book as far ahead as the restaurant allows.
- **A trip that bridges Christmas and New Year's:** Reveillon becomes the Christmas-side anchor, with New Year's Eve as its own separate night out.

## Pro Tips

1. **Book the month before you book the specific date.** Confirm a restaurant is running Reveillon this year before you build a night of the trip around it.

2. **Call, don't click, for a group.** Online reservation systems rarely handle a genuine large-party Reveillon booking correctly — a phone call gets you a real answer on capacity.

3. **Ask about the deposit policy up front.** Group holiday reservations often carry a deposit or a group minimum; know it before the group commits.

4. **Don't assume every course fits every diet.** Fixed menus are harder to modify than à la carte ones — flag dietary needs when you book, not when you sit down.

5. **Pick one night for it, not two.** Reveillon is meant to be the special night, not a nightly habit — save the rest of the trip's dinners for variety.

6. **Old-line vs. modern is a real choice.** A historic institution's Reveillon menu and a newer restaurant's version are genuinely different experiences — decide which fits your group before you start calling.

7. **Confirm start time and pacing expectations.** A multi-course dinner runs longer than a normal meal — build the rest of the evening's plans around a longer table, not a quick bite.

## For a Big Group

Reveillon dinners are one of the easier formal group meals to pull off in New Orleans specifically because the menu is already decided — the coordination problem is capacity, not consensus. A group that would normally spend twenty minutes debating a restaurant and another twenty splitting a complicated bill gets neither problem here: one menu, one price, one table.

The harder logistics are the same ones any December group trip runs into — hotels and large-format properties get tight around the holidays, same as any other high-demand stretch on the calendar. A large-format property with a real kitchen also means Reveillon doesn't have to be the only special meal of the trip; the nights around it can lean on house-cooked dinners instead of a second restaurant reservation. For the largest selection, book 6+ months out.
