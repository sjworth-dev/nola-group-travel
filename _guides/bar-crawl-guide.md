**Pick one zone, go deep on two or three bars, and put one person in charge of headcount.** That's the whole strategy. Everything below is detail.

New Orleans is built for this. Plastic go-cups are standard in the tourist core, late-night bars are everywhere, and the density of drinking spots within a few blocks is hard to match. But a crawl for twenty people is a different animal than one for four. The logistics will eat you alive if you skip them.

This guide gives you routes that work, bars that hold a crowd, and the operational details most crawl lists skip. For the rules on carrying drinks, read the [open container guide](/guides/nola-group-open-container-law-guide/). For the wider night, start with the [nightlife guide](/guides/nightlife-guide/) or the [plan-a-group-trip hub](/guides/plan-a-group-trip/).

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Pick one route (don't try to do the whole city)
- [ ] Designate a wrangler — one person whose job is keeping the group together
- [ ] Cash in everyone's hands before you leave
- [ ] Confirm at least one venue can host your full group for a standing drink
- [ ] Set a meeting-point protocol for anyone who gets separated
- [ ] Eat a real meal before you start
- [ ] Set a "last stop" bar in advance so there's an anchor point

---

## The Fundamental Rule: Pick a Zone

The mistake every large group makes: trying to hit bars in four different neighborhoods in one night. You'll spend half the night in Ubers, lose people in transit, and never settle anywhere.

Pick one zone. Do it well. Go deep on two or three bars rather than shallow on eight.

---

## The Four Zones

### Zone 1: Frenchmen Street (Best Overall)

Three blocks in the Marigny. Walkable. Multiple venues within 50 feet of each other. Live music at every stop. It's where the city's working musicians go to hear each other.

**Why it works for large groups:** You can move between bars without organizing a caravan. Lose someone at The Spotted Cat, find them at d.b.a. thirty seconds later. The street itself is part of the experience.

**The bars:**

| Bar | Vibe | Music | Group Logistics |
|-----|------|-------|-----------------|
| The Spotted Cat | Tiny, sweaty jazz | Live every night | Standing room, get there before it fills |
| d.b.a. | More space, excellent acts | Live nightly, varied genres | Can absorb a larger group |
| Maison | Three floors, rooftop bar | Live bands on multiple floors | Best capacity for big groups |
| Blue Nile | Brass bands, dancing | Late-night energy | Loose, open dance floor |

**The move:** Start at Maison (most capacity, easiest to get drinks), work your way down to d.b.a., end at The Spotted Cat when it's shoulder-to-shoulder and nobody cares.

---

### Zone 2: French Quarter / Bourbon Street (Do It Once)

You're going to Bourbon Street. Accept it. The trick is going with a plan instead of wandering aimlessly until everyone gets separated.

**Why it works for large groups:** Venues built for crowds, and plastic go-cups mean you don't have to stay inside any one bar. The street is the crawl. For a deeper route, see the [Bourbon Street guide](/guides/nola-bourbon-street-guide/) and the [Bourbon Street cocktail tour](/guides/nola-group-bourbon-street-cocktail-tour-guide/).

**Key stops:**

| Bar | Known For | Notes |
|-----|-----------|-------|
| Pat O'Brien's | Hurricanes, dueling piano courtyard | Big venue with a courtyard, built for crowds |
| Lafitte's Blacksmith Shop | Oldest bar in New Orleans, candlelit, cash only | Atmospheric; go early before it gets packed. Bring cash |
| Old Absinthe House | Historic, absinthe cocktails, classic bar | Long bar, decent capacity |
| Tropical Isle | Hand Grenades | Multiple locations on Bourbon, drinks to go |

**The move:** Get a plastic go-cup at the first bar. Do Lafitte's while it's still early and atmospheric. Hit Pat O'Brien's courtyard for the group photo. Don't linger on Bourbon past midnight — move to Frenchmen Street when you're ready for real music.

---

### Zone 3: Craft Cocktail Tour (For the Serious Drinkers)

This one requires Ubers between stops but rewards groups who care more about what's in the glass than the scene.

**Why it works for large groups:** Reservation-friendly bars, excellent service, people actually taste what they're drinking.

**Key stops:**

| Bar | Neighborhood | Known For |
|-----|-------------|-----------|
| Sazerac Bar (The Roosevelt Hotel) | CBD | The Sazerac cocktail, historic hotel bar, impeccable |
| Cure | Uptown (Freret St) | NOLA's benchmark craft cocktail bar |
| Bacchanal Wine | Bywater | Wine, local cheese, live jazz in the courtyard |

**The move:** Start at the Sazerac Bar — it's the most impressive first impression in the city. Take an Uber to Cure for serious cocktails. End at Bacchanal if you want to wind down with live music and wine rather than escalate.

**Logistics note:** Craft cocktail bars move slower. Budget 45-60 minutes per stop minimum. This suits a smaller sub-group that wants quality over quantity; it gets unwieldy with the whole crew, so consider splitting off.

---

### Zone 4: The Full NOLA Arc (Three Acts, One Night)

The classic big-night progression for groups who want to experience multiple sides of the city.

**Act 1 — Early Evening (7-9pm):** Craft cocktails or a proper dinner bar. The Sazerac Bar, the bar at Pêche, or Cane & Table are all excellent entry points.

**Act 2 — Prime Time (9pm-midnight):** Bourbon Street. Walk the strip. Hit Pat O'Brien's. Get it out of your system.

**Act 3 — Late Night (midnight onward):** Frenchmen Street. This is when it gets good. The bands get looser. Stay here until you can't anymore.

## Walk-Around Cups: How It Works

Most visitors misunderstand this rule. In the French Quarter, the written ban is on opened glass containers, so the universal practice is a plastic go-cup. Most bars will pour into one on request. Don't assume it applies in every neighborhood; the [open container guide](/guides/nola-group-open-container-law-guide/) has the details.

**For large groups:** Designate one person to handle the pour-outs before leaving each bar. Twenty people asking individually clogs the bar. One person handling it keeps things moving.

**Don't:** Walk out with a glass. It's what the ordinance bans, and bars won't let you.
**Do:** Ask for a to-go cup every time you're leaving a bar, finish the drink, and start fresh at the next spot.

## Group Logistics: The Operational Details

### Tabs and Money

Open tabs are a nightmare at twenty people. Here's what actually works:

| Method | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|--------|----------|------|------|
| Everyone pays individually | Any size | No complexity | Slow at the bar |
| One person runs the tab | A small sub-group | Fast | Whoever holds the card is at risk |
| Cash rounds | Mid-size crowds | Simple rotation | Someone always forgets to pay back |
| Prepaid arrangement with a venue | The full crew | Clean, fast | Requires planning ahead; see the [bar buyout guide](/guides/nola-group-bar-buyout-guide/) |

**The real answer at full headcount:** Cash. Put $100 in your pocket before you leave. Buy your own drinks. Venmo friends for shared bottles later if you want. Don't try to split 23-way tabs at a crowded bar at 1am.

### Keeping the Group Together

Large groups fragment. This is not a failure — it's physics. Plan for it.

1. **Name a wrangler** — one person who doesn't drink heavily, keeps the headcount, sends location pins
2. **Group chat is active** — pin the chat to the top of everyone's phone before you leave
3. **Set anchor points** — "We'll be at d.b.a. from 11 to midnight. Meet us there."
4. **The buddy system** — pair off. Two people never get truly lost.
5. **Last stop is fixed** — everyone knows where the night ends. This is non-negotiable.

### Getting Around Between Zones

If you're moving between zones, you need multiple [Ubers](/guides/nola-group-ride-share-master-guide/) or a [party bus](/guides/nola-group-charter-bus-guide/). You cannot fit twenty people into four Ubers seamlessly.

**The party bus option:** For large groups doing a multi-zone crawl, a rented shuttle or party bus eliminates the Uber coordination problem. Book ahead. Factor in the cost when you plan — see the [charter bus guide](/guides/nola-group-charter-bus-guide/) for the cost-per-person math against splitting into rideshares.

**Pedestrian routes:** Frenchmen Street to the French Quarter is a 10-15 minute walk along Esplanade. Doable for groups. Bywater to Frenchmen is similar. Anything involving the Garden District, Uptown, or CBD requires vehicles.

## Timing Guide

| Time | What's Happening | Move to |
|------|-----------------|---------|
| 6-8pm | Happy hour, dinner | A proper bar or dinner spot |
| 8-10pm | Early evening, crowds building | Bourbon Street or a craft cocktail bar |
| 10pm-midnight | Prime time, neighborhoods filling up | Frenchmen Street |
| Midnight-2am | Peak energy | Wherever the music is loudest |
| 2am-whenever | Late night core | Wherever is still going; check hours that day |

## Pro Tips

1. **Eat first, eat well.** A real meal before the crawl is not optional for a group. The night lasts longer when nobody's running on empty.

2. **Wear comfortable shoes.** You will walk more than you think. Frenchmen Street is a cobblestone situation. New shoes are a trap.

3. **Cash is king.** ATMs in the French Quarter charge fees that add up. Withdraw before you go out.

4. **Don't try to keep pace with the city.** Many bars stay open late, which gives you permission to stay out forever. That doesn't mean you should. Know your group's limit.

5. **The second night is always better.** If you're here multiple nights, the crawl on night two hits different. You know where things are. You're already oriented. Plan your bigger night for night two.

6. **One anchor bar per zone.** Pick one bar in each zone where you'll regroup if the group fragments. Tell everyone before you leave the house.

7. **Frenchmen Street gets better late.** Resist the urge to arrive at 9pm and leave by 11. Show up later, stay later.

## Where to Stay for the Crawl

This is where large groups hit the wall. Most bars handle a big walk-in better than you'd expect, though a packed one will not. The problem is where 20 people sleep, cook breakfast, and debrief the next morning.

A private villa in the [Bywater](/neighborhoods/bywater/) — such as The Mazant, which sleeps 18 with a heated pool and spa — puts you a short walk from Frenchmen Street and the Marigny. A large-format home in the Lower Garden District or Central City (Heirloom's inventory, or The Revelry's apartment-style floors) trades that walk for easy downtown access without wrestling with Ubers. Either way, a full kitchen for the pre-crawl dinner and the next-morning debrief matters more than most groups plan for. See the [Bywater vs. Lower Garden District comparison](/guides/nola-group-bywater-vs-lgd-deep-comparison/) for the full breakdown.

For a bar crawl trip specifically: if your primary route is Frenchmen Street and the Marigny, a Bywater base is a short walk home. If you're doing more of a French Quarter / Warehouse District crawl, a Lower Garden District base keeps you closer to the action. Other large-format rentals, apartment-style properties like Roami, and hotel room blocks all work too — the neighborhood matters more than the specific property.

## The Night-After Plan

The best part of the crawl happens the morning after.

Get everyone together at the house. Make bloody marys. Reconstruct the night. Half the group will have no idea where they ended up. The other half will have stories.

This is why staying together in a large private rental often beats scattered hotel rooms. The debrief over coffee and eggs is part of the trip.
