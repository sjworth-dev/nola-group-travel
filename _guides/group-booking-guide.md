The verdict: book in a fixed order — house, deposits, private dining, tours, rides — and never book anything for the whole crew until the money is in your account. Every group-trip disaster in New Orleans traces back to skipping one of those steps.

Booking for twenty is not booking for two times ten. The math is different, the failure modes are different, and when you wing it, nineteen people get to watch.

This guide is the operating manual for whoever got volunteered to coordinate. If the trip is still at the "which month, which neighborhood" stage, start with the [group trip planning hub](/guides/plan-a-group-trip/) — it sequences the big decisions. This page picks up once you're actually making calls. It covers restaurants, activities, tours, transportation, and the logistics of getting a large group to show up at the right place at the right time — the [budget guide](/guides/budget-guide/) covers the money side once the bookings are locked.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Designate a single trip coordinator — not a committee
- [ ] Collect a per-person deposit before booking anything
- [ ] Create a shared itinerary document everyone can see (not just you)
- [ ] Lock accommodations first — for the largest selection, book 6+ months out
- [ ] Book private dining rooms and private tours right after the house is locked, not "closer to the date"
- [ ] Confirm headcount with a hard deadline — one change per person after that
- [ ] Set up a group payment system (Splitwise, Venmo, or a shared fund)
- [ ] Get every confirmation number and save it somewhere accessible
- [ ] Build 30-minute buffers into any timed activity
- [ ] Send a pre-trip logistics email 3-5 days before departure

## The Coordinator Role

Somebody has to own this. Not "we'll all share responsibility." Not "anyone can make the call." One person.

That person's job:
- Holds all confirmation numbers
- Makes the final call when the group can't agree
- Sends reminders, not requests
- Is the point of contact for vendors and venues
- Does not apologize for making decisions

If you're reading this guide, that person is probably you. Own it. The trip will be better for it.

## Booking Order

The single biggest mistake groups make: starting too late. The second biggest: booking things in the wrong order. Order matters more than the calendar.

| Step | What to Book | Why This Slot |
|------|--------------|---------------|
| 1 | Accommodations | The address decides which restaurants, tours, and rides make sense. For the largest selection, book 6+ months out. |
| 2 | Deposits from everyone | You don't put a card down for the group until the group has paid you |
| 3 | Private dining rooms | The fewest of them, the most competition — conventions and wedding parties want them too |
| 4 | Private tours, classes, cruises | Most operators can split or add a guide if you ask early |
| 5 | Group transportation | Once you know where you're going and when |
| 6 | Everything else | Brunch, bar tabs, the optional stuff |
| Final week | Confirmations | Call every restaurant and operator 48 hours out |
| 3–5 days before | The pre-trip email | See below |

**Accommodations first, everything else second.** Until the address is locked, nothing else can be properly planned.

### What You're Competing With, by Season

New Orleans demand runs on its own calendar — October is peak here, not shoulder. The season doesn't change the order above; it changes how many other people are working the same list.

| When | Booking Pressure | What It Means for You |
|------|------------------|-----------------------|
| October | Peak | Best weather of the fall, Halloween in the Quarter, conference season. Private rooms go fast. |
| February–May | Peak | Mardi Gras (Fat Tuesday is Feb 9, 2027), French Quarter Fest, crawfish season, Jazz Fest. The hardest stretch of the year to book anything for a crowd. |
| June–August | Soft | Rates drop and crowds thin; Essence Festival over Fourth of July weekend is the exception. Plan indoor backups for afternoon storms. |
| January | Soft | The post-holiday lull before Carnival ramps up. The easiest month to get a private room. |
| Late Aug–mid Sep | Value window | Still hot, before October's ramp — but inside hurricane season's statistical peak. Read the [hurricane season guide](/guides/nola-group-hurricane-season-guide/) before you pay deposits. |

The [best time to visit guide](/guides/nola-group-best-time-to-visit-guide/) goes month by month if the dates aren't set yet.

## Restaurant Reservations at Scale

### The Honest Breakdown

| Headcount | Restaurant Reality |
|-----------|-------------------|
| About a dozen | Some restaurants can seat you in the main room; call ahead, don't book online |
| Mid-to-high teens | You need a private dining room or a restaurant that does large parties routinely |
| Twenty-plus | Private dining room required, or a buyout |
| The whole crew at full headcount | Buyout or event-format restaurants only — or bring dinner to the house |

New Orleans has a strong restaurant culture, and many of the serious restaurants have private rooms or an events coordinator. But you have to ask for the right person. Don't call the host stand. Ask for the events coordinator or private dining manager — the [landmark restaurant booking guide](/guides/nola-group-landmark-restaurant-booking-guide/) has the specifics for the city's highest-demand dining rooms.

### What to Have Ready When You Call

- Exact headcount (or best estimate with a ±2 buffer)
- Date, time, and how flexible you are
- Budget per person (roughly)
- Any dietary restrictions or allergies in the group
- Whether it's a celebration (birthday, bachelorette, anniversary) — restaurants often accommodate with minimal extras
- Who in the group has dietary limits — the [mixed-diet restaurant strategy](/guides/nola-group-mixed-diet-restaurant-strategy-guide/) helps when a gumbo-heavy menu meets a vegan

### Deposits and Cancellation Policies

Large group reservations almost always come with a deposit or credit card hold. Know the policy before you commit.

| Policy Type | What It Means |
|-------------|---------------|
| Credit card hold | Card charged if you no-show or cancel within 24-48 hours |
| Per-person deposit | Charged per head up front, usually applied toward the meal |
| Full prepayment | Sometimes required for private rooms; refund windows vary, so get them in writing |
| Minimum spend | Restaurant requires you to spend $X total or a per-person minimum |

**The smart move:** Put the group deposit on your card, collect the money from the group first. If you wait to collect after the trip, someone always "forgets."

**Read the gratuity line.** Large parties almost always carry an automatic service charge, and set menus often add it on top of a minimum spend. Know before you sign so nobody double-tips at the table — the [tipping culture deep dive](/guides/nola-group-tipping-culture-deep-dive/) covers the local norms.

### Confirmations

Call or email to confirm 48 hours before any large reservation. Restaurants have cancellations and reassignments. A confirmation call puts you back in their memory right before your date.

## Activities and Tours

### Booking Logic

Most NOLA activities are built for a small tour group, not a full takeover. If your group is larger, you have two options:

1. **Book multiple sessions** — stagger your group across two time slots, same day
2. **Book a private tour** — costs more but gets everyone together and usually allows customization

Private tours are worth the premium for anything where group learning matters: history tours, cooking classes, cocktail-making, cemetery tours. A guide shouting over twenty-five people on a sidewalk is not a tour, it's a rumor.

### Activities That Scale Naturally to the Whole Crew

| Activity | Why It Scales |
|----------|--------------|
| [Second line parade (private)](/guides/second-line-guide/) | You hire the band, you set the route |
| Jazz cruise on the Steamboat Natchez | A big riverboat built for crowds; ask about group rates |
| [Swamp tour](/guides/nola-swamp-tour-guide/) | Boat sizes vary by operator; book multiple boats on the same departure |
| Cocktail making class | Private sessions available for full groups |
| Cemetery tour (private) | Own guide, own pace |
| [Cooking class](/guides/nola-cooking-class-guide/) | Several schools run private group classes; confirm the room capacity |

### Activities That Get Difficult Once You Pass Twenty

| Activity | Why It's Hard |
|----------|--------------|
| Restaurant tours | Stops can't seat 25 together |
| Bar tours | Drink service can't keep pace with large groups |
| Museum visits | Self-guided works, guided tours break down |
| Bike tours | Coordinating 25 bikes in traffic |

For activities in the difficult category: split the group. Half does the activity at one time, the other half does something independent. Rotate if needed. This is not failure — it's logistics, and the [split schedule guide](/guides/nola-group-split-schedule-guide/) shows how to run two tracks without the trip fracturing.

## Transportation Coordination

### The Group Transit Problem

The moment you're moving the whole crew anywhere, you have a coordination problem — the [transportation guide](/guides/transportation-guide/) covers the full range of options in more depth than the table below. Standard solutions:

| Option | Best For | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Party bus / charter shuttle | Full-group moves, set schedule | Book once the itinerary is set; cost is shared, usually reasonable per person |
| Multiple rideshares | Flexible timing, shorter distances | Loses people easily; use only when group can split naturally |
| Streetcar | Lower Garden District / Uptown stays | The St. Charles line is the scenic way downtown; not practical for luggage or a group that needs to arrive together |
| Walking | Concentrated neighborhoods | Bywater to Frenchmen, French Quarter loops, Garden District — all walkable |

### Airport Runs

Getting 20 people from MSY to the city is not a rideshare operation — the [airport transfer guide](/guides/nola-airport-transfer-guide/) covers the full logistics. Your options:

1. **Designated shuttle vans** — Book in advance with a group transportation company. They'll have your name at the airport.
2. **Let everyone fend for themselves** — Works if arrivals are spread out, everyone's comfortable with rideshares, and you're not paying as a group.
3. **Staggered rideshares** — Groups of 4-6 per car, everyone knows the address. Not coordinated, but functional.

Whatever villa or hotel block you've booked, the address is your anchor. Send it to everyone the week before so nobody has to ask.

### Moving the Group During the Trip

For nightly outings, the cleanest approach is:
1. Walk where possible
2. Multiple Ubers when you need cars (accept that the group will arrive in waves)
3. Party bus or charter shuttle for anything where the whole group needs to arrive together

The biggest mistake: trying to coordinate 20 people into 5 cars in real time. It takes 30 minutes to get everyone out the door and into vehicles. Build that into your timing.

## Managing Headcount

### The Hard Deadline Rule

Set a headcount deadline and enforce it. After that deadline, the number is locked. Restaurants and tour operators need a number. You can't keep adjusting.

Typical message to the group: "I need your confirmation by [date]. After that, the number is set. If you back out, you're on the hook for any non-refundable deposits."

Put this in writing. Group chats have short memories.

### The Cancellation Problem

Someone will cancel at the last minute. Plan for it.

- **For accommodations:** Large rentals typically have a cutoff after which deposits are non-refundable. Read the policy, then make sure everyone understands it before they commit.
- **For restaurants:** The deposit per person usually covers this — the drop-out loses their deposit.
- **For activities:** Same logic. If you've paid per person and someone drops out, they either find a replacement or eat the cost.

**The collectible deposit model:** The cleanest system is collecting a deposit from every person before you book anything. Size it to cover each person's share of the non-refundable items — add up the deposits you're about to pay and divide. You hold the money, you make the bookings. When the trip ends, you reconcile. The [money management guide](/guides/nola-group-money-management-guide/) goes deeper on kitties and settling up.

## Group Communication

### What Works

| Tool | Use Case |
|------|----------|
| WhatsApp or iMessage group | Day-of coordination, real-time updates |
| Shared Google Doc or Notion page | The master itinerary, all confirmation numbers |
| Venmo / Splitwise | Money collection and tracking |
| Email | Official communication, confirmations |

### The Pre-Trip Email

Send this 3-5 days before the trip. It should contain:

1. Full address of where you're staying
2. Check-in time and check-in procedure
3. Day-by-day schedule (even if rough)
4. All reservation times and locations
5. The group communication channel to use
6. Any dress codes or requirements to know about
7. Where to meet if transportation is arriving separately

This email eliminates 80% of the "wait, what time do we...?" messages.

### Day-Of Coordination

Appoint a point person for each day. It doesn't have to be the same person as the overall coordinator. Day-of coordination is different: it's about herding people, keeping time, and making real-time decisions.

The day-of point person's rule: if the group is not moving in 10 minutes, make the call and start moving. Stragglers catch up.

## Deposits, Payments, and Money

### The Cleanest System

1. Set a total per-person cost estimate before the trip
2. Collect a deposit from everyone that covers non-refundable items
3. Track all shared expenses in Splitwise
4. Do a single reconciliation at the end of the trip
5. One Venmo transaction per person at settlement

### What Not to Do

- Don't let multiple people book things independently without coordinating
- Don't let anyone pay for a group expense and "worry about it later"
- Don't do per-item splitting at restaurants — it takes forever and causes arguments

### The Group Kitty

For trips where there are significant shared expenses (groceries, household supplies, alcohol for the house, tips), a group Venmo or Zelle fund works well. Collect a round number per person upfront. Spend from it throughout the trip. Any remainder gets refunded equally.

## Pro Tips

1. **Over-communicate logistics, under-communicate everything else.** The group doesn't need to know about every decision you made. They need to know when to show up and where.

2. **Confirm everything twice.** Restaurants will lose your reservation. Activity operators will double-book. A confirmation call 48 hours out is non-negotiable.

3. **Name-drop the trip.** When calling restaurants and venues, give your reservation a name. "The Smith group, 22 people." Not just "party of 22." Named reservations get treated differently.

4. **Build the late buffer in.** Whatever time you tell the group, you should actually plan to leave 20-30 minutes later. Someone is always running behind. Build that into the real schedule, not just in your head.

5. **Have a backup for dinner.** Every trip has one night where the original restaurant falls through — reservation lost, kitchen emergency, whatever. Have one good restaurant's number saved that can accommodate walk-ins or same-day calls.

6. **Take photos of all confirmation screens.** Screenshots in a shared album or Google Drive folder. Not just the email confirmations. The screenshots too.

7. **Let go of perfect.** With twenty people, something will go wrong every day. The coordinator who can pivot without stressing is the coordinator everyone wants. Flexibility is a skill.

## For a Big Group

The right base makes every other booking easier. When the whole crew is under one roof, coordinating dinners, getting home after a night out, and feeding everyone breakfast all get simpler. When people are scattered across two buildings, nothing is simple. That's a bias toward one base, stated plainly — a hotel block beats a house when budgets vary or people want their own bill. Past a certain headcount, the [very large group playbook](/guides/group-size-mega-guide/) covers splitting across two properties.

**Editorial examples of what that looks like:** properties like The Mazant (a Bywater guesthouse that sleeps 18) or The Revelry (Central City apartment-style floors, separate billing) put everyone under one roof or one floor and simplify everything else you're coordinating. Roami's downtown apartment-hotel units suit a group near the convention center; see the [convention group guide](/guides/convention-group-guide/) for that scenario. Hotel-block options like The Roosevelt or Hotel Peter & Paul are the right call for a group that wants separate keys and bills. None of these examples is a booking recommendation — they're representative of what the fit looks like; the [hotel vs. villa guide](/guides/hotel-vs-villa-guide/) covers the full field and how to weigh a private house against a hotel room block.

Book your accommodations before you start any other coordination. The address is the anchor that makes every other decision possible.

For the largest selection, book 6+ months out.

## Final Step

Once the accommodations are locked:

1. Send the address to the group immediately
2. Build your restaurant list for that neighborhood
3. Start the activity research knowing where your base is
4. Build your transportation plan outward from your home address

The hardest part of large group booking is managing the humans, not the logistics. Get the structure right and the rest follows.
