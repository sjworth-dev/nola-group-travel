New Orleans is the most popular bachelorette destination in the South, and the reasons aren't mysterious: the city operates like a party you can customize. Street drinks. No last call. Live music you didn't have to plan. Neighborhoods that reward wandering.

Here's the thing nobody tells the maid of honor: the trip is won or lost months before anyone lands, on exactly one decision — where a double-digit group sleeps. Everything else in New Orleans is forgiving. Miss a dinner reservation and you'll still eat well. Skip the swamp tour and nobody remembers. But most rentals in this city cap at 10 guests, so the housing either gets handled early or the party spends the weekend split across three addresses coordinating by group text. This guide covers the actual work, in the order it needs to happen: the timeline, the house, the itinerary hour by hour, the dinner, the money, and the exact messages to send the group.

## Quick Planning Checklist

- [ ] Set a budget early and say it out loud (expect $300–800/person for 3 nights, excluding flights — the honest math is below)
- [ ] For the largest selection of large-group properties, book 6+ months out
- [ ] Pick dates — avoid Mardi Gras unless that chaos is specifically what you want
- [ ] Book accommodations first — large-group options fill before everything else
- [ ] Plan 2-3 key activities and make the reservations before you leave home
- [ ] Make dinner reservations — a big group needs them, especially on weekend nights
- [ ] Coordinate transportation from the airport
- [ ] Create and share a minimal itinerary — the group needs anchor points, not a minute-by-minute schedule

## The T-Minus Timeline

Bach trips die from sequencing errors, not bad ideas. The order:

**6+ months out — dates and the house.** Run the date poll (script below), then book where you're staying the same week the dates settle. For the largest selection, book 6+ months out. Collect the housing deposit before you book, not after — the person who fronts a five-figure hold for a group of fifteen resents it by Tuesday.

**3 months out — the dinner and the anchors.** Book the big dinner (private room if the count is 15+; the [seating strategy guide](/guides/nola-group-large-group-seating-strategy-guide/) covers what to ask), plus one or two reserved activities. Not more. Over-planned bach trips are joyless; New Orleans fills gaps by itself.

**1 month out — logistics.** Airport pickups, the shared itinerary (one page, three anchor points per day, nothing else), spa or blowout bookings if wanted, and the money plan: one Venmo coordinator, everyone pre-loads. The [trip roles guide](/guides/nola-group-trip-roles-assignment-guide/) splits the job so the maid of honor isn't doing all of it.

**Week of — confirmations.** Reconfirm the dinner (call, don't email), place a grocery/liquor delivery order for arrival day, and send the final logistics text: address, door code plan, first-night plan, weather check.

## When to Go

**Best months:** March–May (spring weather, festival energy) and October–November (the year's best weather — but know that October is genuinely peak season here, with pricing to match).

**The value play:** late August through mid-September. Still hot, but rates dip before October's demand ramp — the closest thing this city has to a shoulder season. January is the true bargain window if the group runs cold-tolerant.

**Avoid unless intentional:**
- **Mardi Gras (date moves — Fat Tuesday is February 9, 2027):** Incredible but chaotic, expensive, crowded. Great if that's the plan; overwhelming if you just want a bachelorette trip. The [Mardi Gras group guide](/guides/mardi-gras-group-guide/) is its own project.
- **July–August:** Brutally hot and humid. Plan pool-heavy days and indoor afternoons or pick another month.

**Consider:** **Jazz Fest** (late April–early May) if the bride loves live music, or **French Quarter Fest** (April) — free, high-energy, less crowded. Exact dates for everything are on the [festival calendar](/events/).

## Where Should a Bachelorette Party Stay in New Orleans?

This is the hardest part for a big group, so it goes first and gets decided fast.

### For Large Groups

Once the party goes into double-digit headcount, the regular rental market disappears — most New Orleans short-term rentals cap at 10 guests, by permit. A short list of properties is actually built and licensed for this size: whole-villa compounds in the Bywater, villa clusters near the streetcar, large-format homes across the Lower Garden District, and apartment-style floors with separate billing. The [where-to-stay guide](/where-to-stay/) compares the whole field honestly.

**Why a whole house wins for bach parties:**
- A pool to yourselves for day drinking and the group photo
- Everyone stays together (no coordinating between hotels)
- Full kitchen for the welcome party and morning mimosas
- Space to spread out when someone needs a break from the group

**Before you pay any deposit, get three things in writing:** the permitted guest count (not the marketing "sleeps 20"), a real bed breakdown (beds versus pullouts and air mattresses — someone always ends up on the loser bed, and she should at least know in advance), and whether it's one connected property or separate units on different blocks.

### For Groups of 10 or Fewer

You have the whole market. Search the [Bywater](/neighborhoods/bywater/) (trendy, walkable, artsy), the [Marigny](/neighborhoods/marigny/) (next to Frenchmen Street nightlife), or the [French Quarter](/neighborhoods/french-quarter/) (central, loud at night — decide if that's a feature).

## The 3-Night Itinerary, Hour by Hour

Anchor points, not a schedule. Three commitments a day; New Orleans handles the rest.

### Day 1: Arrival

- **3:00pm** — Check in, claim rooms (decide the room-assignment method *before* arrival; "first cab from the airport wins" starts wars)
- **4:00pm** — Grocery and liquor delivery arrives (ordered the week before — nobody wants a supermarket run in going-out clothes)
- **5:00pm** — Pool, drinks, decorations, the sound of a weekend starting
- **8:00pm** — Welcome dinner at the house: order in from a local spot (Pizza Delicious travels well) or walk to a wine garden like Bacchanal if you're Bywater-based
- **11:00pm** — Stop. This is the discipline night. The [drink pace guide](/guides/nola-group-drink-pace-management-guide/) exists because night one is where bach trips blow their Saturday.

### Day 2: The Big Day

- **10:00am** — Brunch out (The Country Club, Atchafalaya's Bloody Mary bar) or a slower house brunch with a hired-in option
- **12:30pm** — The chosen afternoon: spa/blowouts, pool cabana, Magazine Street shopping, or a swamp tour (funnier with a hangover than you'd think)
- **4:00pm** — Back to the house. Nap window. Enforce it gently.
- **7:30pm** — *The* dinner (below). Photos before, not after.
- **10:00pm** — Frenchmen Street for live music (The Spotted Cat if you can pack in, d.b.a. or Maison for room to dance) — or Bourbon Street if the group wants the circus. Both are correct answers for different groups.
- **1:00am+** — The group naturally splits into night owls and pumpkin coaches. Plan for it: everyone knows the address, nobody walks home alone, rideshares in twos and threes.

### Day 3: Recovery + Round Two

- **10:30am** — Slow start. Beignets at Café Du Monde (yes, it's touristy; do it anyway) or Bloody Marys at the house
- **1:00pm** — Low-key: Garden District wander, Magazine Street boutiques, or nothing at all by the pool
- **7:00pm** — Casual dinner near the house, then Bacchanal-style wine-and-live-music night, or a burlesque show (The Allways Lounge) if the group has one more gear
- Earlier night. Flights exist.

### Day 4: Departure

House breakfast, pack-out assignments (one person owns the fridge, one owns lost-and-found sweep), staggered airport runs.

## The Dinner

One dinner should be *the* dinner. For a big group that means a private room or a dedicated section, booked well ahead — walk-ins do not happen at this size.

- **Commander's Palace** – The classic. Private rooms, turquoise everything, 25-cent martinis at lunch. The marquee choice.
- **Compère Lapin** – Caribbean-influenced, stylish; ask about the back room.
- **Cochon** – Southern/Cajun, handles groups well.
- **Pêche** – Seafood, James Beard winner, big space.

**The call script** (phone, not OpenTable, for anything over 10): *"Hi — I'm planning a bachelorette dinner for [X] people on [date], around 7:30. Do you have a private room or a section that fits us? Is there a set menu for groups, and is it one check? What deposit do you need to hold it?"* Four questions, ninety seconds, and you'll know if it's the spot. The [restaurant guide](/guides/restaurant-guide/) has the full private-dining rundown and what to do when the first choice is booked.

**Brunch:** The Country Club (pool + brunch, made for bach parties), Atchafalaya (build-your-own Bloody Mary bar — book the patio), Willa Jean (trendy, bakery-forward).

## Bachelorette Activities

**Classic NOLA:** French Quarter walking tour · cemetery tour · Steamboat Natchez cruise · haunted history tour

**Pool:** The Country Club (reserve a cabana) · your own rental — if the house has a pool, the day plans itself

**Pampering:** Spa day (Belladonna, Nola Bliss) · group mani-pedis (book ahead) · blowout bar (Drybar Uptown)

**Only-in-New-Orleans:** cooking class (gumbo or beignets) · swamp tour (alligators, airboats, group screaming) · burlesque show (The Allways Lounge) · a [second line parasol workshop](/guides/nola-group-second-line-umbrella-making-guide/) if the group wants a craft with a payoff

## Nightlife Guide

**French Quarter:** Bourbon Street (loud, trashy, fun — and correct in small doses) · Pat O'Brien's (Hurricanes, dueling pianos) · The Carousel Bar (classy; literally rotates)

**Frenchmen Street (better than Bourbon):** The Spotted Cat (tiny, sweaty, incredible) · d.b.a. (real music room) · Maison (three floors)

**Bywater:** Bacchanal Wine (wine garden, live music, perfect) · BJ's Lounge (dive, authentic)

## How Much Does a New Orleans Bachelorette Party Cost?

Per person, three nights:

| Category | Budget | Mid-Range | Splurge |
|----------|--------|-----------|---------|
| Accommodations | $75-100 | $125-175 | $200+ |
| Food & Drinks | $150-200 | $250-350 | $400+ |
| Activities | $50-100 | $100-200 | $200+ |
| Transportation | $30-50 | $50-75 | $75-100 |
| **Total** | **$300-450** | **$525-800** | **$875+** |

*Excludes flights.*

Worked example for a crew of twelve at mid-range: roughly $525–800 each, so the organizer is moving $6,000–$9,500 of other people's money. That's why the money mechanics matter as much as the bookings:

- **Collect before you book.** Deposit first, reservation second. Every time.
- **One coordinator, one ledger.** A single Venmo/Splitwise pool, pre-loaded per person, topped up once mid-trip. Twenty-way check splits at the table are how dinners end badly.
- **Budget the forgotten lines:** cleaning fees on the rental, gratuities (a big-group dinner will add 20% automatically), the bride's share (the group covers her — say so in the first message so it's priced in), and one round of "we need a second party bus" contingency.
- **What people forget to say out loud:** the budget itself. Send the per-person number *before* anyone books flights. The awkward conversation is much cheaper in month one than at checkout.

## The Messages to Send (Copy, Paste, Adjust)

**The date poll (6+ months out):** *"NOLA bach for [bride]! 🎉 Three possible weekends: [A], [B], [C]. Vote by Friday — majority wins, no revotes, calendar sympathy will be noted but overruled."*

**The money message:** *"Budget check: this trip runs about $[X]/person for the weekend covering the house, the big dinner, and activities — flights separate, and we're splitting the bride's share. Deposit of $[Y] to [name]'s Venmo by [date] locks your spot. House gets booked as soon as deposits land."*

**The week-of logistics text:** *"Final details: address is [X], arrive after 3pm. Day 1 = pool + welcome night at the house. Saturday = the big one — dinner 7:30, wear [theme]. Sunday = recovery + round two. Weather says [X]. Bring: swimsuit, comfortable shoes, cash for cash-only bars."*

## When Your Group Is Over 10 People

If you cross into the large-group range, logistics change:

- **Accommodations:** only a short list of properties legally fits a double-digit group — compare them in the [where-to-stay guide](/where-to-stay/) and book before anything else
- **Restaurants:** Call ahead. A group this size needs private dining or a reserved section. The [large group seating strategy guide](/guides/nola-group-large-group-seating-strategy-guide/) covers what to ask when you call and how to set up seating for 15+ without a private room.
- **Transportation:** Two rideshares minimum, always. Consider a party bus for the big night, and remember drinks don't ride along — open containers in vehicles are a real violation, not a vibe.

## Pro Tips

1. **Book accommodations first.** Large-group options book up fast, and everything else can flex.

2. **Make dinner reservations by phone.** Especially for a double-digit table. Do it weeks ahead, reconfirm the week of.

3. **Designate a Venmo coordinator.** One person collects money and pays for group things. The [trip roles guide](/guides/nola-group-trip-roles-assignment-guide/) splits logistics, restaurants, and finance across three people so nobody burns out.

4. **Build in downtime.** Some of the best moments happen by the pool, and the Saturday nap window saves the Saturday night.

5. **Pack comfortable shoes.** You'll walk more than you expect, on sidewalks with opinions.

6. **Bring cash.** Some of the best bars are cash-only.

7. **Don't over-plan.** Three anchors a day. New Orleans rewards spontaneity, and the [drink pace guide](/guides/nola-group-drink-pace-management-guide/) keeps night one from stealing the weekend.
