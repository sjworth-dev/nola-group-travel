**Verdict: go, but treat it as a full day out at the lakefront, not a stop on the way to somewhere else.** It is the best food-first festival on the spring calendar, and the cause makes the line for brisket feel less guilty.

Most of the spring festival calendar pulls a group toward the French Quarter or the Fair Grounds. Hogs for the Cause pulls the other direction entirely — out to the UNO lakefront, where dozens of amateur and professional barbecue teams spend a weekend competing over pork, smoke, and a genuinely good cause: raising money for families dealing with pediatric brain cancer.

It's part cookoff, part music festival, and part backyard-party-at-scale — teams build elaborate booth setups, live bands play across the grounds, and the smell of smoked pork carries over the whole lakefront. For a group that wants a festival day built around food and music without the density of Jazz Fest or the [French Quarter Fest](/guides/french-quarter-fest/) crowds, this is a different kind of spring day.

Hogs for the Cause lands squarely inside the city's spring [peak season](/guides/festival-season-calendar/), so treat it with the same booking urgency as anything else on the March calendar — it's not a secret off-season find.

## What Hogs for the Cause Actually Is

**The format.** A charity barbecue competition where teams — ranging from serious competitive pitmasters to enthusiastic amateurs — set up elaborate booths and compete for prizes, while the event runs live music on stages across the grounds for the full weekend.

**The cause.** The festival exists to raise money for families navigating a child's pediatric brain cancer diagnosis, and that mission runs through the event rather than sitting off to the side of it — a meaningful amount of the weekend's spirit comes from what it's actually funding.

**The venue.** The event runs on the grounds around the UNO Lakefront Arena, a different footprint from the French Quarter or Fair Grounds events most group trips are built around — open lakefront space rather than a dense historic neighborhood.

**The scale.** Multiple days, dozens of competing teams, and a genuinely large crowd — this isn't a quiet neighborhood block party like the [Freret Street Festival](/guides/nola-group-freret-street-festival-guide/). It's a full festival production, just with barbecue and brass bands instead of a single headline act.

| Hogs for the Cause | Jazz Fest / FQF |
|---|---|
| BBQ-competition-first, music alongside | Music-first |
| UNO lakefront grounds | Fair Grounds / French Quarter |
| Charity mission built into the event | No charity throughline |
| Ticketed | FQF free / Jazz Fest ticketed |
| Multi-day, open-grounds format | Multi-day, larger citywide footprint |

## Running It With a Group

### Treat the Team Booths Like a Tasting Crawl

The real draw is working the grounds team by team, sampling different takes on barbecue rather than committing to one plate at the first booth your group finds. If your crew has already done a [po-boy crawl](/guides/nola-po-boy-crawl-guide/), the same instinct applies — small portions, wide sampling, compare notes as a group.

### Plan the Trip Out to the Lakefront

Unlike a French Quarter or Uptown festival day, this one requires an actual trip to the lakefront. Build in transportation time and coordinate as a group rather than assuming everyone can walk or grab a quick rideshare the way they might from a Bywater or Marigny villa. The [transportation guide](/guides/transportation-guide/) covers group logistics for getting across the city.

### Sun and Shade Planning

The lakefront grounds are largely open. For a group spending a full day outside, plan for sun exposure the way you would for any outdoor day festival — hats, sunscreen, a shaded rally point if the grounds have one, and water breaks built into the day rather than left to chance.

### Pair It With the Rest of the Spring Calendar

Because Hogs for the Cause lands in March, it falls after [Mardi Gras](/guides/mardi-gras-group-guide/) (Fat Tuesday is February 9 in 2027) and ahead of [French Quarter Fest](/guides/french-quarter-fest/) and [Jazz Fest](/guides/jazz-fest-group-guide/). Check the [events calendar](/events/) for how the weekends stack. A group building a longer spring trip can treat it as one distinct day inside a bigger festival-season itinerary rather than the whole reason for the trip.

## Pro Tips

1. **Confirm the dates before booking anything else.** The 2027 weekend is announced as March 19–20, but dates move year to year — check the official site before you commit.

2. **Buy tickets in advance for the group.** A charity festival with this kind of following doesn't guarantee easy gate access for a big group showing up unplanned.

3. **Bring cash alongside cards.** Team booths vary in how they handle payment; cash keeps the line moving.

4. **Don't skip the transportation plan.** The lakefront isn't walkable from most group lodging — treat this like any other cross-town festival day.

5. **Pace the eating across teams.** The whole point is sampling widely — don't fill up at the first booth with the longest line.

6. **Watch the forecast and plan for sun.** This is a mostly open-air, unshaded event footprint.

7. **Remember this lands in spring peak season.** March sits inside the city's high-demand stretch — book lodging and transportation early.

## For a Big Group

Hogs for the Cause is an easy full-day anchor for a group that wants a festival built around food, music, and an actual cause rather than pure spectacle — no dense historic-neighborhood crowd to navigate, and a genuinely different energy than the French Quarter or Fair Grounds events most spring trips default to.

Because it sits inside the city's spring peak season alongside Mardi Gras, French Quarter Fest, and Jazz Fest, plan lodging and transportation like any other March or April weekend. For the largest selection, book 6+ months out.
