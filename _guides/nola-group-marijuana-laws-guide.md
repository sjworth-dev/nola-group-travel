**No, marijuana is not legal in New Orleans — but possession of 14 grams or less is decriminalized.** Under Louisiana law, small-amount possession is a summons and a fine of up to $100 — no arrest, no jail, for the first offense and every one after it. New Orleans goes a step further: the city automatically forgives its own possession summonses. What remains fully illegal: buying or selling it, holding more than 14 grams, smoking in public, and bringing any amount near the airport.

That gap between "decriminalized" and "legal" matters more for a group of fifteen than it does for one person traveling alone. This guide covers where the real lines sit, the two legal-ish ways THC actually gets bought here (there are two, and neither is a dispensary run), and how the picture changes depending on whether your group is staying in a villa-style rental or a hotel.

## Quick Planning Checklist

- [ ] Understand the core distinction: decriminalized possession of up to 14 grams, not legal possession
- [ ] Don't assume public consumption is fine anywhere—smoking in public carries its own, separate risk
- [ ] If someone in the group holds a valid medical marijuana card from home, they can legally buy at a Louisiana-licensed pharmacy — but everyone else can't
- [ ] Never bring anything through MSY airport security or on a flight—airports are federal property and federal law doesn't recognize any state's decriminalization
- [ ] If staying at a villa, treat consumption as an indoor, private-property matter, not a porch or street activity
- [ ] If staying at a hotel, check the property's own no-smoking policy before assuming decriminalization covers you there too—hotels can and do charge cleaning fees regardless of what the city or state allows
- [ ] Assign a sober "house driver" the same way you would for alcohol—driving under the influence of marijuana is enforced the same as driving drunk

## Is Weed Decriminalized in New Orleans?

Yes — and the numbers are specific. Since August 2021, Louisiana state law treats possession of **14 grams or less** as a misdemeanor punishable only by a fine of **up to $100**, enforced by a summons — the paperwork equivalent of a traffic ticket — rather than an arrest. Unlike many states' versions, this isn't a first-offense-only grace: the same cap applies to repeat offenses.

New Orleans layers its own policy on top: the city council directed that municipal possession summonses be **automatically forgiven**, so within the city the practical consequence of a small-amount possession stop rounds to zero. That's the reality behind the city's relaxed reputation.

The cliff is at 14 grams. Above that — or anything that looks like distribution — the conversation leaves "decriminalized" entirely and enters real criminal territory, with penalties that scale up fast. A group pooling a supply in one bag is the easy, avoidable way to cross that line by accident.

**Decriminalized still does not mean legal.** The conduct remains against the law; the consequence is what changed. That distinction is why everything else in this guide still matters.

## Can You Smoke Weed in Public in New Orleans?

No. Public consumption is a separate issue from possession, and it's the one that actually draws enforcement. There is nothing like the [open container allowance for alcohol](/guides/nola-group-open-container-law-guide/) for marijuana — smoking on Bourbon Street, a Frenchmen Street sidewalk, or in a park is not covered by any decriminalization, and it's the most visible possible version of the conduct. Groups often assume the alcohol rule and the marijuana rule work the same way. They don't.

Indoors isn't automatically safe either: New Orleans' smoke-free rules and, more practically, every hotel's own no-smoking policy apply regardless of what the possession law says. A hotel hallway or balcony is a shared, semi-public space — it puts you back in the public-consumption category even though it feels private.

## Can You Buy Weed in New Orleans?

There is no recreational dispensary system in Louisiana — no legal retail path for a visitor to buy marijuana, period. But two adjacent things are legal, and it's worth knowing exactly what they are because half the confusion on this topic comes from conflating them:

**1. Medical marijuana — including for visitors with an out-of-state card.** Louisiana's medical program runs through licensed pharmacies, and since 2022 the law has allowed a "visiting qualifying patient" — someone holding a valid medical marijuana card issued by another state — to purchase at a Louisiana-licensed pharmacy with that card and a photo ID. If someone in your group is a medical patient at home, they have a legal route here. (Flying home with it is still a federal problem — plan around that.) Everyone without a card has no purchase path at all.

**2. Hemp-derived THC drinks and edibles.** The THC seltzers and low-dose gummies sold openly around New Orleans are federally-legal hemp products, and Louisiana regulates rather than bans them: since January 2025, servings are capped at **5 milligrams of THC**, packages at 40 mg, sales are 21-and-up at permitted retailers only (gas stations can no longer carry them), and inhalable hemp products are banned outright. A 5 mg seltzer is a genuinely mild product — closer to a light beer than to anything a dispensary sells. One caution for trips in late 2026 and beyond: a federal law taking effect in November 2026 redefines hemp in a way that's expected to pull most of these products from shelves, so check what's actually available rather than building plans around it.

If a member of your group already has product with them, that's a possession question — governed by the 14-gram math above — not a shopping trip your group can plan around.

## Where This Applies (and Where It Doesn't)

| Situation | Reality |
|-----------|---------|
| Possession of 14 grams or less | Decriminalized statewide — summons and up to $100 fine; New Orleans forgives its own summonses |
| Possession over 14 grams, or anything resembling distribution | Real criminal exposure — a different category entirely |
| Buying or selling | No recreational retail path exists in Louisiana |
| Medical marijuana | Louisiana patients — and visitors with a valid out-of-state medical card — via licensed pharmacies |
| Hemp-derived THC drinks/edibles (5 mg servings) | Legal, 21+, at permitted retailers; rules tightening again in late 2026 |
| Smoking in public (streets, sidewalks, parks) | Not covered by decriminalization — separate enforcement risk |
| Smoking inside a private villa rental | Private property; the rental's own policy governs it, not city ordinance |
| Smoking inside a hotel room | Almost always against the hotel's no-smoking policy — expect a cleaning fee or worse |
| MSY airport and other federal property | Federal law applies; decriminalization does not extend here at all |
| Driving after consuming | Enforced the same way as driving under the influence of alcohol |

The pattern to notice: nearly every row that reads "not covered" or "hard line" involves a public space, a purchase, or federal jurisdiction. The decriminalization applies narrowly — to possession, in small amounts, in private. Everything adjacent to it sits outside.

## Villa vs. Hotel: Why It Plays Out Differently

This is where group logistics actually matter, more than the letter of the law does.

**At a villa-style rental**, your group has a private, enclosed space with its own kitchen, living areas, and outdoor space. If consumption is happening, it's happening indoors, away from the street, and away from a hotel hallway full of other guests. That doesn't change what's legal—public consumption is still public consumption whether it's a sidewalk or a shared courtyard—but it does mean a group has more actual control over keeping the whole question private and contained. A rental in the [Bywater](/neighborhoods/bywater/) or the [Lower Garden District](/neighborhoods/lower-garden-district/) gives a group a real living room and a real front door, which matters here the same way it matters for keeping a bar-crawl group from drifting into a residential block with open containers.

**At a hotel**, you're dealing with a second layer of rules that has nothing to do with city or state law: the hotel's own smoking policy. Nearly every hotel in New Orleans is a no-smoking property, and that policy is enforced through housekeeping, not police—a strong smell in a room is a fast way to get a cleaning fee charged to the card on file, independent of whether the underlying possession was decriminalized. A hallway or balcony is a shared, semi-public space, which puts you back in the "public consumption" category even though it feels private.

The practical takeaway for a group organizer: a private house gives a big group more room to manage its own behavior, the same way it does for noise, guest counts, and late-night comings and goings. It doesn't make anything more legal. It makes the group's exposure more within its own control.

## What Actually Gets Enforced

1. **Public consumption**, far more than quiet possession, is what draws police attention—smoking openly on a street or in a park reads very differently to enforcement than a bag in a bag.
2. **Driving under the influence** is treated exactly like alcohol-impaired driving, with the same real consequences. The [rideshare guide](/guides/nola-group-ride-share-master-guide/) covers how to move a group around at night without anyone needing to be behind the wheel.
3. **Anything at the airport or on federal property** is enforced under an entirely different, stricter framework that the city's decriminalization has no effect on.
4. **Quantities beyond 14 grams** move the conversation out of "decriminalized possession" territory and into distribution-adjacent risk, which is a different and more serious category entirely. A group pooling its supply in one place is the accidental version of this.
5. **Combining it with other visible disorder**—the same pattern as public intoxication—draws more attention than any one factor alone.

## Pro Tips

1. **Don't confuse decriminalized with legal when you're briefing the group.** Set the expectation correctly before the trip: up to 14 grams is a forgiven-summons situation in the city; everything else is not.

2. **Keep it indoors, at the rental, full stop.** Whatever your group decides to do privately, doing it in a shared or public space is the actual legal exposure—not the possession itself.

3. **Never bring any amount near MSY.** Airport security operates under federal rules that don't care what the city or state allows. Leave it behind.

4. **If someone has a medical card from home, bring it.** A valid out-of-state medical marijuana card plus photo ID works at Louisiana-licensed pharmacies — the one genuinely legal purchase path a visitor has.

5. **If the group wants the legal-adjacent version, the 5 mg hemp seltzers are it.** Sold at permitted shops, 21+, mild by design. Verify what's on shelves if your trip is after late 2026 — the federal rules are changing.

6. **If you're at a hotel, assume the no-smoking policy is real and enforced.** A cleaning fee is the best-case outcome; treat the room like any other no-smoking space.

7. **Assign a sober driver, same as you would for a night of drinking.** Driving under the influence carries the same real risk regardless of substance.

## Large Groups and This Rule

A group of fifteen or twenty behaves differently than one or two people, and that matters here the same way it matters for open containers or noise. A couple stepping outside a hotel for a minute is a non-event. A visible group doing the same thing on a balcony or in a hotel hallway reads very differently, and it's the kind of thing that draws a policy violation or a complaint fast. And a group's pooled stash does the 14-gram math a lot faster than one person's does.

This is one more argument for a whole-house base over scattered hotel rooms for a group this size. A private house with its own indoor space gives the group room to manage its own choices without turning a private matter into a public or semi-public one. It's the same logic that applies to loud music, late arrivals, and everything else that's easier to contain inside four walls and a real front door than across a shared hotel corridor.
