Most of this site is built around events that ask something of your group — standing in a crowd, drinking through the afternoon, chasing a parade route. Celebration in the Oaks doesn't ask much of anyone. It's a holiday light display spread across City Park, running through December, and it's one of the few things in New Orleans that works equally well for a grandparent, a toddler, and a group of adults who just want a quiet, pretty evening between louder days.

That's the actual case for it. A December trip to New Orleans already has a strong anchor in [Reveillon dinners](/guides/nola-group-reveillon-dinner-guide/) and the holiday food scene covered in the [holiday gathering guide](/guides/holiday-gathering/), but those are dinner-table events. Celebration in the Oaks is the walk-around, low-commitment evening that fills the gap between them — especially for a multigenerational group where not everyone wants a late night out.

Here's how it actually works and how to fit it into a group's December week.

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Confirm this year's exact operating dates, hours, and ticket prices on the City Park website — the display's specific run and pricing tiers are announced closer to the season
- [ ] Decide whether your group is walking the display, riding the train/carousel add-ons, or doing a drive-through option if one is offered that year — check current-year format details officially
- [ ] Buy tickets in advance for a weekend evening; midweek is generally the easier walk-up option
- [ ] Dress for a December evening walk — Louisiana winter evenings are cool, not cold, but you're outside for a while
- [ ] Treat it as a 90-minute-to-2-hour evening activity, not a full night out
- [ ] Pair it with a nearby Mid-City dinner before or after rather than trying to eat inside the display footprint

## What Celebration in the Oaks Actually Is

Celebration in the Oaks is City Park's annual holiday lights display, running through the month of December. City Park is already one of the more underused parts of the city for group trips — the [Mid-City/City Park full-day guide](/guides/nola-group-mid-city-city-park-full-day-guide/) covers the daytime version of the park — and the holiday display transforms sections of it into a lit walking (and in past years, ride-through) experience.

**The format.** Millions of lights strung through the park's oak canopy and gardens, typically combined with seasonal displays, some food and drink vendors, and rides for the display's family-oriented sections. Confirm the current year's specific configuration and any ticketed add-ons before you go — formats and offerings can shift year to year.

**The pace.** This is a walk-through (or drive-through, in some years) event on its own clock, not a standing-room festival. A visit runs a couple of hours at most, which makes it easy to slot into an otherwise busy December evening.

**The crowd.** Family-oriented and low-disruption compared to almost anything else on this site's calendar. There's no equivalent to a festival crush or a parade crowd here — it's a pleasant walk, not an event you need a strategy to survive.

> Don't plan around a specific date, price, or ride lineup from memory — confirm the current year's hours, ticketing, and offerings on the official City Park site before buying anything.

## How It Fits a December Group Trip

December in New Orleans isn't classified as a demand peak the way October or the February–May stretch are, but it has its own dedicated anchors: Reveillon dinners, [New Year's Eve](/guides/new-years-guide/), and now Celebration in the Oaks as the family-friendly evening option.

| Evening option | Commitment | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Celebration in the Oaks | Low — a couple hours, walk-around | The whole group, especially with kids or older relatives along |
| Reveillon dinner | High — a full seated multi-course meal | The food-focused subset of a December trip |
| New Year's Eve in the Quarter | High — a full night, crowds, noise | Groups that want the big countdown night |

A group doesn't need to choose one lane for the whole trip. Celebration in the Oaks works well as the low-key night early in a December week, saving the higher-commitment nights — a Reveillon dinner, NYE itself — for later in the stay.

## Running It With a Group

### Use It as the Multigenerational Night

If your December trip mixes generations — a family reunion, a milestone trip with parents along — this is the event that doesn't require splitting the group by energy level or drinking preference. Nobody needs a plan B for the kids or the grandparents; it's built for everyone to do together.

### Buy Tickets for a Specific Evening

Because it's a ticketed, dated event rather than a free wander-in festival, decide on a night in advance rather than assuming you'll swing by whenever. Weekend evenings draw bigger crowds; a midweek night is the easier call for a group that wants a calmer walk.

### Don't Overbuild the Evening Around It

At most two hours of actual display time. Plan dinner before or after at one of Mid-City's restaurants rather than trying to make a full night out of the display itself — it's a strong complement to an evening, not the evening's whole structure.

### Combine It With a Daytime City Park Visit If the Schedule Allows

If part of your group is already doing [a full City Park day](/guides/nola-group-mid-city-city-park-full-day-guide/) — NOMA, the Sculpture Garden, the kayak launch — returning that same evening for the lights is an easy way to get a full day and a full night out of one park visit instead of two separate trips.

## Pro Tips

1. **Confirm current-year hours and format before booking anything.** The display's exact dates, ticket tiers, and any ride or drive-through options are announced closer to the season and can change year to year.

2. **Buy ahead for weekend nights.** Midweek evenings are the more reliable walk-up option if your group's schedule is flexible.

3. **Dress for a cool evening, not a cold one.** Louisiana December evenings are mild by most standards but still worth a jacket for a couple hours outside.

4. **Treat it as a two-hour add-on, not a full evening plan.** Pair it with dinner in Mid-City rather than trying to stretch the display itself into a whole night.

5. **This is the event that doesn't split your group by age or energy.** Use it deliberately on a night when you want everyone together rather than splitting into subsets.

6. **Combine it with a daytime City Park visit if your schedule allows.** One trip to the park can cover both the day and the night version of it.

7. **Don't assume December booking pressure mirrors the festival calendar.** December isn't part of the city's classified peak season, but popular dates around the holidays and New Year's Eve still fill up — plan lodging early regardless.

## For a Big Group

Celebration in the Oaks earns a real place on a December group itinerary precisely because it doesn't demand anything from the group — no crowd strategy, no drink pacing, no splitting by who wants to stay out late. For a multigenerational reunion or a family trip where not everyone wants the same kind of night, it's a rare event that keeps the whole group together without anyone compromising.

Because it's a short, low-disruption evening, accommodation strategy for a December trip is driven more by the other anchors — Reveillon dinners, New Year's Eve — than by this display specifically. A central or Mid-City-adjacent base makes the park an easy add-on either way. Spread the search across the property field by fit rather than fixating on one name.
