New Orleans might be the best bachelor party city in America. No last call. World-class food. Live music everywhere. And unlike Vegas, nothing here charges a cover to be interesting.

Here's what nobody tells the best man: the trip is decided months out, on one decision — where a double-digit crew sleeps. Everything else in New Orleans forgives you. Miss a reservation and you'll still eat absurdly well. Skip the swamp tour and nobody remembers. But most rentals in this city cap at 10 guests by permit, so the house either gets handled early or the party spends the weekend split across three addresses. This playbook runs the actual work in order: the timeline, the house, the itinerary hour by hour, the food, the money, and the exact messages to send the group.

## Quick Planning Checklist

- [ ] Set the budget out loud early (expect $350–1,000/person for 3 nights excluding flights — honest math below)
- [ ] For the largest selection of large-group properties, book 6+ months out
- [ ] Pick dates against the [festival calendar](/events/) — a random weekend here can be Jazz Fest pricing
- [ ] Book the house first; everything else flexes
- [ ] Book the day-2 activity (golf, charter, swamp) before you land
- [ ] Reserve 2 dinners by phone — a big table is a call, not an app
- [ ] Assign roles: one guy on money, one on reservations, one on transport
- [ ] Share a one-page itinerary — anchor points, not a minute-by-minute schedule

## The T-Minus Timeline

Bachelor trips die from sequencing errors, not bad ideas. The order:

**6+ months out — dates and the house.** Run the date poll (script below), then book the house the same week dates settle. For the largest selection, book 6+ months out. Collect deposits *before* booking — the guy who fronts five figures for the crew resents it by Tuesday.

**3 months out — the activity and the dinners.** Lock the day-2 centerpiece: tee times for a big group take real lead time (the [large group golf guide](/guides/large-group-golf-guide/) covers booking as a crew), charters cap at 4–6 per boat so a big group needs several (the [fishing guide](/guides/nola-group-fishing-guide/) covers the split), and swamp tours want a group reservation. Book the two big dinners by phone the same week.

**1 month out — logistics.** Airport pickups, the one-page itinerary, gambling plans if any, and the money plan: one Venmo coordinator, everyone pre-loads. The [trip roles guide](/guides/nola-group-trip-roles-assignment-guide/) splits the job so the best man isn't doing all of it.

**Week of — confirmations.** Reconfirm dinners by phone, place a grocery/liquor delivery for arrival day, send the final logistics text (template below).

## Where to Stay

This is the hardest part for a big crew, so it goes first and gets decided fast.

Once the party goes double-digit, the regular rental market disappears — most New Orleans short-term rentals cap at 10 guests by permit. A short list of properties is actually built and licensed for this size: whole-villa compounds in the Bywater, villa clusters near the streetcar in the Lower Garden District, large-format homes, apartment-style floors with separate billing, and hotel group suites. The [where-to-stay guide](/where-to-stay/) compares the whole field honestly, and the [hotel vs. villa guide](/guides/hotel-vs-villa-guide/) settles the one structural question.

**Why a whole house wins for bachelor parties:**
- A pool and a grill make day two plan itself
- Poker night costs nothing and beats a $400 club table
- Full kitchen for the recovery breakfast that saves day three
- Nobody coordinates between hotels at 2am

**Before any deposit, get three things in writing:** the permitted guest count (not the marketing "sleeps 20"), the real bed breakdown (beds versus pullouts — someone's getting the loser bed and should know), and whether it's one property or separate units on different blocks.

**Neighborhood logic:** [Bywater](/neighborhoods/bywater/) is quieter, artsy, and good for a home-base trip; the [Lower Garden District](/neighborhoods/lower-garden-district/) is central with streetcar access; the French Quarter is where you'll be anyway — you may not want to sleep there too. The [neighborhood comparison guide](/guides/nola-group-neighborhood-comparison-guide/) runs the full matchup.

## The 3-Day Itinerary, Hour by Hour

Anchor points, not a schedule. Three commitments a day; New Orleans fills the rest. (Want a version to hand the group? The [bachelor party itinerary guide](/guides/bachelor-party-itinerary/) is the printable skeleton.)

### Day 1: Arrival

- **3:00pm** — Check in, claim rooms (decide the method before arrival; "first Uber from the airport wins" starts wars)
- **4:00pm** — Grocery and liquor delivery arrives (ordered the week before)
- **5:00pm** — Pool, cards, the sound of a weekend starting
- **8:00pm** — Dinner: Cochon (meat-heavy, built for groups) or keep it at the house with a po-boy run
- **10:00pm** — Frenchmen Street for live music
- **1:00am** — Stop. This is the discipline night. The [drink pace guide](/guides/nola-group-drink-pace-management-guide/) exists because night one is where bachelor trips lose their Saturday.

### Day 2: The Main Event

- **8:00am** — The activity: golf (tee times below), a fishing charter fleet, or the airboat swamp tour ([the deep-dive](/guides/nola-group-airboat-deep-dive-guide/) says bring sunglasses and a sense of humor)
- **1:00pm** — Back to the house. Pool, grill, pre-game.
- **3:00pm** — Nap window. Enforce it. The nap is the difference between a legendary Saturday night and a 10pm casualty.
- **7:30pm** — *The* dinner (below). One good group photo before, not after.
- **10:00pm** — The Bourbon Street lap (once, ironically, get it out of the system) then Frenchmen for the real music
- **1:00am+** — The crew splits into night owls and pumpkin coaches. Plan for it: everyone knows the address, rideshares in twos and threes, and Verti Marte exists for a reason at 3am.

### Day 3: Recovery + Round Two

- **10:30am** — Slow start. The [hangover recovery guide](/guides/nola-group-villa-hangover-recovery-guide/) covers stocking the house for exactly this morning. Beignets at Café Du Monde if anyone can move.
- **1:00pm** — Bloody Marys and a burger (Port of Call, cash only), a Saints or Pelicans game if the calendar cooperates, or nothing at all by the pool
- **7:00pm** — Low-key dinner near the house, then Bacchanal-style wine-garden night or one more Frenchmen set if the group has another gear
- Earlier night. Flights exist.

### Day 4: Departure

House breakfast, pack-out assignments (one guy owns the fridge, one owns the lost-and-found sweep), staggered airport runs.

Book big-group tee times weeks out — the [large group golf guide](/guides/large-group-golf-guide/) covers the mechanics.

### On the Water

- **Fishing charter:** half-day redfish and speckled trout runs. Boats take 4–6, so a big crew books a small fleet — the [fishing guide](/guides/nola-group-fishing-guide/) covers the split and the marina logistics.
- **Swamp/airboat tour:** alligators guaranteed, funnier with a mild hangover than you'd expect.
- **Kayaking Bayou St. John:** the quiet option nobody suggests and everybody enjoys.

### Other Ideas

- **Gambling:** Harrah's (now Caesars New Orleans) downtown — the city's one land-based casino floor, with a sportsbook. Manage expectations: it's a casino, not a Vegas resort ecosystem.
- **Saints/Pelicans game:** if timing works, nothing beats the Superdome with a crew.
- **Gun range:** shooting sports available outside the city for the group that wants it.

## Where to Eat (Group-Friendly)

Reserve at least three weeks out for a double-digit table, by phone. **The call script:** *"Hi — I'm planning a bachelor party dinner for [X] on [date], around 7:30. Do you have a private room or a section that fits us? Set menu for groups? One check or split? What deposit holds it?"* Four questions, ninety seconds. The [restaurant guide](/guides/restaurant-guide/) has the full private-dining rundown and the [seating strategy guide](/guides/nola-group-large-group-seating-strategy-guide/) covers what to do when the first choice is booked.

## Nightlife Guide

**Bourbon Street (do it once):** trashy, loud, touristy — and you're going anyway. Pat O'Brien's (Hurricanes, dueling pianos), Lafitte's Blacksmith Shop (oldest bar in the country, cash only), Cat's Meow (karaoke, unreasonably fun). Get it out of the system night one or as the day-2 lap.

**Frenchmen Street (the real move):** three blocks of live music where the city actually goes. The Spotted Cat (tiny, sweaty, incredible) · d.b.a. (real music room) · Maison (three floors) · Blue Nile (brass bands). The [bar crawl guide](/guides/nola-group-bywater-bar-crawl-guide/) covers moving a big crew between rooms without losing half of it.

**Know the street rules before the first go-cup:** plastic yes, glass no — the [open container guide](/guides/nola-group-open-container-law-guide/) is the two-minute read that keeps the night simple.

## How Much Does a New Orleans Bachelor Party Cost?

Per person, three nights:

| Category | Budget | Mid-Range | Baller |
|----------|--------|-----------|--------|
| Accommodations | $100-150 | $175-250 | $300+ |
| Food & Drinks | $200-300 | $350-500 | $600+ |
| Activities | $50-100 | $150-250 | $300+ |
| Transportation | $30-50 | $50-75 | $100+ |
| **Total** | **$380-600** | **$725-1075** | **$1300+** |

*Excludes flights and gambling losses, which are yours to explain.*

Worked example for a crew of twelve at mid-range: roughly $725–1,075 each, so the organizer is moving $9,000–13,000 of other people's money. The money mechanics matter as much as the bookings:

- **Collect before you book.** Deposit first, reservation second. Every time.
- **One coordinator, one ledger.** A single Venmo/Splitwise pool, pre-loaded, topped up once mid-trip. Twelve-way check splits at the table are how dinners end badly.
- **Budget the forgotten lines:** rental cleaning fees, auto-gratuity (a big-group dinner adds 20% automatically), the groom's share (the group covers him — say so in the first message so it's priced in), and one "second van" contingency.
- **Say the number early.** Send the per-person cost *before* anyone books flights. The awkward conversation is cheaper in month one than at checkout.

## The Messages to Send (Copy, Paste, Adjust)

**The date poll (6+ months out):** *"[Groom]'s NOLA send-off. Three weekends on the table: [A], [B], [C]. Vote by Friday — majority rules, no revotes, your calendar is your problem."*

**The money message:** *"Budget check: the weekend runs about $[X]/person — house, big dinner, [activity] — flights separate, groom rides free. $[Y] deposit to [name]'s Venmo by [date] locks your spot. House gets booked the day deposits land."*

**The week-of logistics text:** *"Final details: address [X], arrive after 3pm. Friday = pool + Frenchmen. Saturday = [activity] 8am (yes, 8am), dinner 7:30. Sunday = recovery. Weather says [X]. Bring: swimsuit, one collared shirt (Commander's), cash for cash-only bars."*

## Pro Tips

1. **Pace yourselves.** No last call is a trap. The [drink pace guide](/guides/nola-group-drink-pace-management-guide/) has an actual framework, and the Saturday nap is non-negotiable.

2. **Eat real food.** Don't just drink. New Orleans food will save you — that's not a metaphor.

3. **Bring cash.** Several of the best bars are cash-only, and ATM fees inside them are predatory.

4. **Rideshare everywhere.** Nobody drives. Open containers don't ride along either — that's a real violation, not a vibe.

5. **Activity first, then wrecked.** Golf, boats, and swamps happen morning of day 2, before anyone is a casualty.

6. **Assign the jobs.** Money, reservations, transport — three guys, not one. The [trip roles guide](/guides/nola-group-trip-roles-assignment-guide/) has the structure.

7. **Tip well.** The service industry runs this city. Don't be those guys.
