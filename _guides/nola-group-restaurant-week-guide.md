January is the one month on the calendar where New Orleans isn't fighting a festival, a parade, or a heat wave for your attention — and Restaurant Week is the city's answer to what to do with that lull. For roughly a week in mid-January, a wide swath of the city's best restaurants run special prix-fixe menus at a reduced price, and a group that eats seriously can get a meaningfully cheaper version of a splurge dinner it would otherwise save for a bigger night.

This lands inside the city's genuine soft season — see the [festival season calendar](/guides/festival-season-calendar/) for why January is the post-holiday lull before things ramp back up toward Mardi Gras — which means lower lodging rates on top of lower dinner tabs. It's a real value window, not a marketing one.

This isn't a festival with a footprint or a ticket. It's a citywide reservation exercise, and for a group, the strategy is entirely about booking, not about showing up. Here's how to actually run it.

---

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Confirm this year's exact dates and participating restaurant list on the official Restaurant Week site — the roster and dates are announced closer to January each year
- [ ] Decide as a group how many Restaurant Week dinners you actually want, not just how many nights you're in town
- [ ] Call participating restaurants directly to confirm they can seat your group size on the prix-fixe menu — not every restaurant runs the format the same way for large parties
- [ ] Book the moment reservations open; the best-known participants fill their prime evening slots fast even at a discounted price point
- [ ] Ask each restaurant whether the prix-fixe is the only menu available that week or an option alongside the regular one
- [ ] Have a backup restaurant in mind per night in case your first choice can't accommodate the full group

## What Restaurant Week Actually Is

Restaurant Week isn't a single event with a gate or a ticket. It's a citywide format: for about a week in mid-January, participating restaurants — ranging from casual neighborhood spots to some of the city's most notable dining rooms — offer a fixed multi-course menu at a set, reduced price instead of (or alongside) their regular menu.

**The format.** Typically a two- or three-course prix-fixe at a price point well below what the same restaurant's tasting or a la carte menu would run. The specific courses and price vary by restaurant and by year — confirm the current lineup before you build a night around a specific spot.

**The timing.** Mid-January, landing squarely in the city's soft season. Hotels and short-term rentals are at their least competitive time of year, restaurants are hungry for January business after the holiday lull, and the format exists specifically to give locals and visitors a reason to go out during the month everyone else treats as a lull.

**The catch for groups.** A prix-fixe menu built around a fixed per-person price is a very different operational reality for a restaurant than its usual seating. Some participating restaurants cap Restaurant Week party sizes lower than they would for a normal reservation, since the kitchen is running a tighter, higher-volume format that night. Always ask directly rather than assuming a restaurant that normally handles large groups will do so at the same size during Restaurant Week.

> Don't lock in a specific restaurant, menu, or price from memory — confirm the current year's participants, dates, and pricing on the official Restaurant Week site before booking anything.

## Booking Strategy for a Group

| Approach | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| One shared Restaurant Week dinner | The whole group books one restaurant together for one night | Smaller groups, or a crew that wants one anchor dinner without splitting logistics |
| Split across restaurants, same night | The group breaks into smaller parties, each booking a different participating restaurant | Larger groups where no single restaurant can seat everyone on the prix-fixe |
| Multiple nights, different restaurants | The group books a different Restaurant Week dinner each night it's in town | Groups treating Restaurant Week as the trip's food anchor rather than a single meal |

The larger the group, the more Restaurant Week actually rewards splitting up. A prix-fixe dinner is built around turning tables at a set price, and a restaurant that would happily seat twenty people on a normal Tuesday may only be able to take eight or ten on the Restaurant Week menu. Don't force one reservation onto a headcount it can't hold — split into two or three tables at the same or different restaurants and regroup after.

## Running It With a Group

### Book the Week Reservations Open, Not the Week You Arrive

Restaurant Week reservations open before the event itself, and the well-known participating restaurants — the ones every local food writer names first — fill their best evening slots within days. If your group has a specific restaurant in mind, that's a task for the moment the list and booking window go live, not a call you make once you've landed.

### Call, Don't Just Book Online for Large Parties

Online reservation systems are usually built around parties of two to six. For anything larger, call the restaurant directly, state your exact headcount, and ask specifically whether they can run your group on the Restaurant Week menu. This is the same large-party logic covered in the [large-group restaurant seating guide](/guides/nola-group-large-group-seating-strategy-guide/) — it applies just as much to a discounted prix-fixe week as it does to a normal Saturday night.

### Treat It as a Value Window, Not a Compromise

Because January sits in the city's soft season, Restaurant Week dinners pair naturally with lower lodging costs and a quieter city overall. This isn't the trip to expect festival energy or packed streets — it's the trip where a group eats extremely well, spends less doing it than any other month, and has the city mostly to itself between dinners. If a slower January trip appeals to your group, the [budget guide](/guides/budget-guide/) breaks down how the rest of a January visit's costs compare to peak season.

### Don't Skip the Backup Restaurant

Because prix-fixe party-size caps are real and restaurant-specific, have a second and third choice restaurant ready per night before you start calling. A group that only researches one option per evening risks losing the week's window entirely if that one restaurant can't accommodate the full headcount.

## Pro Tips

1. **Confirm this year's dates and participant list before planning anything.** Restaurant Week's roster and exact dates are announced closer to January and change year to year.

2. **Call large parties in directly.** Don't assume an online booking widget built for a party of four will process your group's actual headcount on the prix-fixe menu.

3. **Book the moment reservations open.** The best-known participating restaurants sell out their prime Restaurant Week evenings fast, discount or not.

4. **Expect to split across tables or restaurants at real group size.** A prix-fixe format often caps party sizes lower than a restaurant's normal group capacity.

5. **Have backups per night.** Research a second and third restaurant option before you start calling, so one full reservation book doesn't sink the night.

6. **Lean into the value window.** January's soft-season rates apply to lodging as much as dinner — this is a genuinely cheaper version of a New Orleans group trip.

7. **Ask if the prix-fixe is the only menu offered that night.** Some restaurants run it alongside their regular menu; others switch entirely for the week. It affects what you can actually order.

## For a Big Group

Restaurant Week works differently for a big group than almost anything else on the January calendar — it's not an event you attend together so much as a citywide reservation puzzle you solve together. The group that wins is the one that starts calling restaurants the day the participant list goes live, accepts that the headcount may need to split across tables, and treats "everyone eats well" as the goal rather than "everyone eats at the same restaurant."

Because January is soft season, accommodation is the one part of this trip that isn't a scramble. A central base near the restaurant corridors your group is targeting cuts down on transportation between dinners, and January's lower demand means more flexibility in property choice than you'd have during [Jazz Fest](/guides/jazz-fest-group-guide/) or [Mardi Gras](/guides/mardi-gras-group-guide/). Spread the search across the property field by fit rather than fixating on one name.

For the largest selection, book 6+ months out.
