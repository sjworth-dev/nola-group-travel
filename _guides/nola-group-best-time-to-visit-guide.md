Every generic "best time to visit" list defaults to the same script: spring and fall are shoulder season, summer is brutal, book the shoulder months for the best mix of weather and value. None of that applies here.

New Orleans runs its own calendar. October is the best weather of the year and the fullest pricing to match it — not a secret bargain month. Summer is genuinely hot, and it's also the closest thing this city has to a real discount window. Spring isn't shoulder season at all; it's Mardi Gras, French Quarter Fest, and Jazz Fest stacked back to back, which makes February through May the single longest peak stretch most destinations don't have.

This guide exists because that inverted calendar is scattered across a dozen other pages on this site — the [festival calendar](/guides/festival-season-calendar/), the [shoulder season guide](/guides/shoulder-season-guide/), the [October guide](/guides/nola-group-october-guide/) — but never assembled into one place a group organizer can use to actually pick a month. Here it is, straight through.

## The Calendar, Inverted

Most U.S. destinations follow a simple rhythm: summer is peak, spring and fall are shoulder, winter is quiet. New Orleans doesn't follow it, and treating it like it does is the single most common planning mistake group organizers make here.

**Peak season is October, and February through May.** October is peak because of weather, not a festival calendar quirk — it's the first real relief from summer heat, plus Voodoo Fest and a Halloween scene the whole city leans into. February through May is peak because it's Mardi Gras, French Quarter Fest, spring crawfish season, and Jazz Fest, one after another with barely a gap.

**Soft season is June through August, and January.** Summer means heat, humidity, and afternoon thunderstorms that clear out both crowds and rates — real value, if your group can handle the weather. January is the lull after the holidays and before Mardi Gras ramps up: the year's cheapest rates, full city operations, cold snaps possible.

**The genuine shoulder window — the one other cities call "shoulder" by default — is late August through mid-September.** Still hot, but ahead of October's demand ramp and past the worst of peak summer.

## Don't Confuse Risk Season With Value Season

This trips up more organizers than any other seasonal question. Hurricane season runs June through November, with August through October the statistical peak of that window. It is not the same thing as the pricing calendar.

October sits inside hurricane season and is simultaneously the most expensive, most in-demand month of the year. Late August also sits inside hurricane season and is one of the cheapest. Storm risk is a real planning factor — travel insurance, flexible dates, a plan B — but it tells you nothing about whether a given month is a bargain or a splurge. Check both separately.

## How to Choose: Match the Month to What Your Group Actually Wants

**Group wants the best weather and doesn't mind paying for it:** October. Full stop — nothing else in the calendar matches the temperature and the energy at once.

**Group wants a specific festival:** Build the trip around the festival's dates, not the season. Mardi Gras and Jazz Fest both move annually — confirm the current year's dates before you book anything else.

**Group is budget-primary:** Late January or late August through mid-September. Real savings, not marketing-speak savings.

**Group wants quiet, walkable streets over festival energy:** Late January. Fewer visitors, full restaurant availability, a city running at its own pace.

**Group can't handle heat or humidity:** Anything except June through September. If summer is the only window that works schedule-wise, plan the day around the heat rather than around activities.

## Pro Tips

1. **Don't default to "spring and fall are shoulder season."** That's true almost everywhere except here. Spring is New Orleans' single busiest, most expensive stretch.

2. **Check the current year's Fat Tuesday date before anything else.** It moves by weeks year to year, and it reshapes February and March's pricing and crowd levels depending on where it lands.

3. **October's "no discount" reality surprises more organizers than any other seasonal fact about this city.** Budget for peak pricing if you're going for the weather.

4. **If your group is heat-sensitive, don't romanticize "just go in the shoulder season."** Late August is still genuinely hot — the discount is real, but so is the heat index.

5. **Book large-group accommodation 6+ months out no matter the month.** Villas and houses that hold a big group under one roof are a limited category of inventory, not a limited-time sale.

6. **Layer your priorities before you pick a date.** Weather, budget, and a specific festival rarely all line up in the same weekend — decide which one your group cares about most.

7. **Watch the hurricane-season calendar separately from the pricing calendar.** They overlap in months but measure completely different things.

## For a Big Group

Timing gets more consequential the bigger the group is. A couple can decide on a whim and find a hotel room almost any week of the year. A large group sharing one house doesn't have that flexibility — the properties that hold the whole crew under one roof are a small, finite category, and they book out furthest ahead during exactly the months this guide flags as peak.
